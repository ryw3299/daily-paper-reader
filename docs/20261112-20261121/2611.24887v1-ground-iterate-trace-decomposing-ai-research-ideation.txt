# ABSTRACT 

Large language models have been widely used to generate plausible research ideas, yet it remains unclear what makes automated ideation effective and how generated novel proposals arise from prior scientific work. We introduce the Cre-ative AI Researcher (CAR) , an agentic framework combining literature ground-ing, iterative development and explicit conceptual operators. CAR constructs a semantic knowledge graph from retrieved literature and equips an ideation agent with graph-retrieval tools and four operators: bisociation, analogy, exploration and elaboration. Each operator call is checked against structural acceptance conditions and recorded, producing a trace of intermediate transformations and declared lit-erature sources. We evaluate CAR with two language-model backbones across 40 LiveIdeaBench research topics and two execution benchmarks. CAR achieves significantly higher methodological novelty (MN) than single-shot prompting and The AI Scientist. Its methodological novelty does not differ significantly from the Research Ideation Agent, while its feasibility scores are higher on both back-bones. With a shared experiment executor, CAR achieves the highest pairwise win rate among the evaluated AI methods on FML-bench-Lite and BioXArena. In a ten-condition ablation study, MN point estimates favor CAR over one-step proposal on both backbones, but no MN contrast survives correction for multi-ple comparisons. The results do not establish a consistent quality benefit from explicit operators beyond free-form iteration. CAR makes the sequence of oper-ations and declared literature sources inspectable, separating judged idea quality from procedural traceability. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

AI scientists, LLM agents that propose research ideas, implement them and report the results, have moved from demonstrations to systems whose papers are submitted for peer review (Lu et al., 2026). In these systems, ideation is either a brief step before execution or is interleaved with it, as the agent proposes its next change to a working implementation. How the idea is formed matters for what follows: given the same executor, replacing its own ideas with ideas formed separately changes the outcome (§5.2). Furthermore, the diversity of an agent’s ideas predicts its research performance on MLE-bench (Audran-Reiss et al., 2025). Current ideation agents fall short in two opposite ways. Agents prompted to be bold produce ideas that experts judge more novel than human ideas but slightly weaker on feasibility (Si et al., 2025), and the same agent is the least feasible of those we compare (§5.1). The ideation stages of end-to-end AI scientists sit at the other end: run on their own, they produce the most feasible ideas we measure and, for The AI Scientist on GPT-5.4, the least original. These contrasting profiles motivate evaluating originality and effectiveness together, the two criteria of the standard definition of creativity (Runco & Jaeger, 2012). We address the step before implementation: forming a research proposal by transforming the con-cepts and relations found in prior work. Theories of creativity describe such transformations, among them bisociation (Koestler, 1964), analogy (Gentner, 1983), exploration of a conceptual space (Bo-den, 2004) and elaboration (Guilford, 1967). In LLM ideation, such processes are often elicited through prompting (Gu et al., 2024; Shen et al., 2026), while some systems also retain structured intermediate representations. This motivates distinguishing the instruction to make a creative move 1arXiv:2611.24887v1 [cs.CL] 18 Nov 2026 Preprint. Under review. from an explicit check of its form. Ideation agents also bundle retrieval and multi-round refinement into one pipeline (Si et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2024), making their individual contri-butions difficult to disentangle. This leaves two questions open: which parts of an ideation process improve its ideas, and how a given idea arose from the literature. We introduce the Creative AI Researcher (CAR) . CAR extracts a semantic knowledge graph of problems, methods, results, claims and limitations from the literature retrieved for a topic. An ideation agent then develops a proposal over several rounds, using graph-retrieval tools and the four processes above, which CAR exposes as operators that edit the graph. Each operator has a structural definition that is validated before the edit is applied. The accepted and rejected operations, and the literature entities the final proposal declares, form a trace for every idea. CAR allows us to vary iterative development, operator availability, validation and relation labels within one implementation. We evaluate it on 40 LiveIdeaBench topics with two LLM backbones and execute its ideas with a shared experiment agent on FML-bench-Lite and BioXArena. CAR has higher methodological novelty (MN) than single-shot prompting and The AI Scientist. Its MN differences from the Research Ideation Agent (RIA) (Si et al., 2025) are not significant, while its feasibility scores are higher on both backbones. CAR also obtains the highest pairwise win rate among AI methods on both execution benchmarks. The ablation MN point estimates favor CAR over one-step proposal, but do not establish a consistent quality benefit from explicit operators beyond free-form iteration. The operators provide a recorded sequence of graph edits and declared literature sources. Our contributions are: • A decomposition of AI research ideation into literature grounding, iterative development and ex-plicit creative operators. A ten-condition study on two backbones examines iteration and the op-erator layer within this grounded setting, distinguishing their effects on judged idea quality from the procedural trace they provide. • CAR, which makes the decomposition testable: four creativity processes implemented as vali-dated, recorded operators over a literature-derived knowledge graph, so that every proposal carries a trace from prior work to the final idea. • An evaluation of CAR on originality and effectiveness, the two criteria of the standard definition of creativity, on three benchmarks, with judge agreement assessed against expert ratings. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 GROUNDING IDEATION IN THE LITERATURE 

Research ideation systems ground ideas in prior work (Wang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025; Si et al., 2025; Baek et al., 2025) or through a graph, which selects or forecasts concept pairs (Gu & Krenn, 2024; 2025), conditions generation (Li et al., 2026b; Lei et al., 2025), supplies reasoning paths (Buehler, 2024; Ghafarollahi & Buehler, 2025), is extended by the model as it reasons (Buehler, 2025b;a), or holds the ideas themselves, as in Evolving Idea Graphs (Dong et al., 2026). Evolving Idea Graphs also validates candidate graph edits. CAR focuses on four creative operators with explicit acceptance conditions, recording their accepted and rejected calls as ideas develop. 2.2 ITERATIVE AND AGENTIC IDEATION 

Chain of Ideas (Li et al., 2025) and Nova (Hu et al., 2024) retrieve and refine seed ideas over several rounds, multi-agent teams assign roles and debate (Baek et al., 2025; Ghafarollahi & Buehler, 2025; Swanson et al., 2025; Gottweis et al., 2025; Su et al., 2025), and end-to-end AI scientists couple ideation to experiment search and writing (Lu et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2025; Weng et al., 2026), with evidence from MLE-bench that ideation diversity predicts performance (Audran-Reiss et al., 2025). Many of these systems develop ideas through natural-language revision and critique before or during experimental execution. Trace: the proposal is stored with its declared literature entities and its accepted and rejected operations. 2.3 CREATIVE OPERATORS AND TRACEABLE REASONING 

CAR’s operators are named after bisociation (Koestler, 1964), with a bridge entity from conceptual blending (Fauconnier & Turner, 2003), structure-mapping analogy (Gentner, 1983), exploratory creativity (Boden, 2004; Wiggins, 2006) and elaboration (Guilford, 1967). Earlier computational work includes analogy mining (Hope et al., 2017) and bisociative networks (Ahmed & Fuge, 2018), CHIMERA catalogs recombinations in published papers (Sternlicht & Hope, 2025), and LLM work elicits such moves through prompting (Radensky et al., 2026; Gu et al., 2024; Shen et al., 2026) or implements combinational, exploratory and transformative reasoning over generated thoughts (Suzuki & Banaei-Kashani, 2025). Some approaches retain intermediate representations: Radensky et al. (2026) record purpose, mechanism and evaluation facets, while Shen et al. (2026) generate and assess explicit analogies. Such artifacts make the proposed transformations inspectable, but do not establish that the final idea depends on them. This distinction also arises in the faithfulness problem of chain-of-thought (Turpin et al., 2023; Lanham et al., 2023). Lyu et al. (2023) make reasoning faithful by having an LLM translate a problem into a symbolic representation, which a deterministic solver executes to obtain the answer. CAR applies a related principle at the tool boundary: each creative operator must satisfy structural acceptance conditions before its graph edit is applied. The resulting trace records the operations performed, without establishing their causal necessity to the final proposal. 2.4 EVALUATING AI-GENERATED RESEARCH IDEAS 

Benchmarks pair LLM judges with fixed rubrics (Ruan et al., 2026; Qiu et al., 2025) or test whether judges predict later research outcomes (Ye et al., 2026). Retrieval-grounded checks compare an idea against its nearest published work (Si et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Shahid et al., 2025). Whether LLMs are qualified originality reviewers is contested (Huang et al., 2025). We assess agreement between our originality and methodological novelty judges and the expert ratings of Si et al. (2025) and an adjusted human-agreement reference, with development-set limitations discussed in Appendix D.

3.1 KNOWLEDGE CONSTRUCTION 

Corpus. For each research task, an LLM queries the Semantic Scholar API in an iterative loop, with search prompts adapted from the Research Ideation Agent (Si et al., 2025): keyword queries, queries for papers similar to a given paper, and queries for a paper’s references (Appendix K.1.1). An LLM scores the retrieved papers for relevance and we retain up to 20 papers with the highest relevance scores among those whose full text can be obtained. 

Extraction. From each paper an LLM extracts typed entities, each with a technical description, and the relations between them: the paper itself, and its problems, tasks, concepts, assumptions, methods, implementation details, hyperparameters, materials, experiments, metrics, results, claims, limitations and formulas (Appendix K.1.2). This follows a view from scientometrics that a paper’s elements are the substrate of original recombination (Dirk, 1999; Luo et al., 2022; Zhao & Zhang, 2025). Extracting them as distinct typed entities makes recombination expressible as an explicit operation on the graph. The result is a directed multigraph G = ( V, E ) with E ⊆ V × R × V , where 

R is the relation vocabulary produced by extraction. We write Vlit = V for these literature nodes and Vpaper ⊂ Vlit for paper nodes, which record provenance only. Two repair steps add relations for insufficiently connected entities and merge nodes with matching normalized names and entity types. Neither step introduces new entities (Appendix A). The graph is built once per topic and frozen. Each idea starts from this graph, with relation labels permuted in the relation-label-shuffle ablation. 3.2 GRAPH RETRIEVAL 

The ideation agent reads the graph through three tools. ask knowledge graph exploration agent delegates a natural-language question to a graph exploration agent adapted from ARK (Polonuer et al., 2026), which searches the graph by keyword and over one-hop neighborhoods and returns relevant entities with its reasoning for each. get entity details returns an entity’s full description given its index, and get edges by entity lists its incident literature edges. 3.3 RESEARCH IDEATION WITH CREATIVE OPERATORS 

Creative operators. The ideation agent develops an idea through four creative operators, each an explicit edit to the graph that is validated before it is applied and recorded afterwards. Let 

Gt = ( Vt, E t) be the graph after t accepted operations within one idea, with G0 = G, and let 

Vprop ,t = Vt \ Vlit be the entities proposed so far. Every proposed edge (s, r, o ) must carry a written description of why s and o stand in relation r (which need not belong to R), every new entity must carry a type and a technical description, and no operation may name a paper node: it must use the specific problem, method, result or claim. An operation that meets its condition in Table 1 is applied. Otherwise, the agent receives an error naming the violated condition and must revise it or choose another move. 

Operator design. Bisociate (Koestler, 1964) connects two existing, unlinked entities through a new 

bridge entity. The one-edge form (s, r, o ) is not allowed, since it only asserts a relation between two concepts the literature already contains, whereas the bridge forces a new concept at their intersection (Fauconnier & Turner, 2003). Analogize follows structure mapping (Gentner, 1983): it carries a relation from a source pair to a target pair, and only the target is added to the graph. The source may come from the agent’s own knowledge, since restricting it to the corpus would confine transfer to the retrieved literature. The target must change both endpoints: keeping one fixed ( A : B → C : B)does not carry the relation to a new pair, it only adds C as another entity related to B. Explore ,4Preprint. Under review. after Boden’s exploratory creativity (Boden, 2004), extends the graph by one step from an existing entity, which may itself be proposed. Elaborate , after Guilford’s elaboration (Guilford, 1967), adds no entity: it replaces a proposed entity’s description with a more specific one and may tie it to a literature entity through one grounding edge. Every operation therefore attaches to the graph at one or more entities, which is what makes an idea traceable. 

Proposal. The agent is instructed to complete at least three accepted operations before submitting a proposal. The proposal follows the idea format of The AI Scientist-v2 (Lu et al., 2026) and additionally declares the literature entities it builds on as node indices in Vlit . We store each idea with its accepted and rejected operations, their arguments and their errors, which together with the declared entities form the trace of the idea (Appendix G). Proposed entities and edges are discarded before the next idea, so every idea starts from G0.

# 4 EXPERIMENTS 

We frame evaluation with the standard definition of creativity. We first assess (a) the originality and 

feasibility of CAR’s ideas, and then go beyond feasibility, which is necessary but not sufficient for creativity, to assess (b) their effectiveness as test-metric improvement when executed. 4.1 BENCHMARKS AND TASKS 

LiveIdeaBench (Ruan et al., 2026) contains 1,180 scientific keywords from 22 domains and uses LLM judges to assess the ideas generated for a keyword along dimensions inspired by Guilford’s account of divergent thinking (Guilford, 1967): originality , feasibility , clarity (how understandable and complete an idea is) and fluency (how distinct a method’s ideas for one keyword are from one another). We select 40 keywords, ten each from the computational, physical, life and social sciences. We report its fifth dimension, flexibility, in Appendix E.2. Our modifications to the protocol are listed in Appendix C.1. 

FML-bench (Zou et al., 2026) comprises 18 machine learning research tasks in 10 domains, each with a baseline repository, a set of editable files and a held-out test metric, and scores an agent on how far it improves the baseline. All agents edit code and run experiments through the same benchmark-provided tools, so differences between agents reflect their research strategy rather than their tooling. We use the Lite split of eight tasks. 

BioXArena (Li et al., 2026a) is a benchmark of 76 end-to-end biomedical machine learning tasks spanning nine areas, most combining several input modalities. Unlike FML-bench, a task supplies data and a metric but no baseline repository, so an agent must design the pipeline and the method. We use the ten tasks of the benchmark’s human-comparison pilot, which supplies a human reference. One task, cell-type-from-expression, has a label leak that makes it trivially solvable (five of seven entries, including CAR and the human reference, score 1.000 ). We show it but exclude it from means and win rates. 4.2 EVALUATION METRICS 

LiveIdeaBench. We follow the benchmark’s LLM-as-a-judge protocol, with its prompts, scales and fluency score map. A panel of three critics from vendors other than the generators’ scores each idea 

1–10 for originality (O), feasibility (F) and clarity (C), and judges fluency (Flu.) on every pair of a method’s five ideas for each keyword. Because a critic judges originality from memory, we add a methodological novelty (MN) judge that grounds the judgment in retrieved prior work. Each idea is converted to an approximately 250 -word abstract. Related papers are retrieved from Semantic Scholar with the iterative query loop of Si et al. (2025) under a publication-date cut-off, ranked by its LLM relevance score with ties broken by SPECTER2 similarity (Singh et al., 2023), and the top 10 are kept. The same three judges then apply the Stratified Novelty Judge of Shen et al. (2026), adapted from biomedical to general research, rating 0–10 how much of the proposed methodology has already been applied to the idea’s research task in the retrieved papers, and we average the three ratings as the idea’s overlap. LiveIdeaBench. We compare CAR with a single-shot Base LLM (the backbone prompted once with the benchmark’s own idea prompt), the Research Ideation Agent (RIA) (Si et al., 2025), and the ideation stages of The AI Scientist and The AI Scientist-v2 (Lu et al., 2026). Each baseline generates in its native format and is normalized afterwards (Appendix B). RIA draws its retrieved papers from CAR’s corpus of up to 20 papers for the keyword, The AI Scientist-v2 runs its own Semantic Scholar search, and The AI Scientist’s ideation stage uses no literature. Every method runs on GPT-5.4 and GLM-5.2, and two numbers given without labels are for these backbones in this order. To lower cost, CAR’s graph exploration agent uses DeepSeek-V4-Pro, and the ideation agent may send it only one natural-language query per idea. We generate up to five ideas per keyword. 

FML-bench and BioXArena. CAR’s proposals are supplied to the executor of The AI Scientist-v2, adapted to BioXArena’s time limits (Appendix C.3). We compare CAR with The AI Scientist-v2 using this shared executor and with the methods reported by each benchmark. CAR and The AI Scientist-v2 use GPT-5.4, as do the FML-bench agents, while the BioXArena baselines keep the benchmark’s own model specifications. We generate three ideas per run, with three runs on FML-bench and one on BioXArena, following each benchmark’s protocol.

# A KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPAIR AND ENTITY RESOLUTION 

Per-paper extraction can leave scientific entities without relations to other non-paper entities and can represent the same entity under different names. We apply relation repair within each paper and entity resolution across the assembled graph. 

Relation repair (within a paper). We measure relation coverage as the fraction of non-paper nodes incident to at least one edge whose endpoints are both non-paper nodes. Edges incident to paper nodes record provenance and are excluded from this measure. When coverage falls below 80% , an LLM proposes additional relations using the extracted entity summaries and existing relations, with up to 20 currently unconnected entities highlighted in the prompt (Appendix K.1.3). A candidate relation is accepted only if both endpoints resolve to entities already extracted from that paper, its relation label and description are present, and its subject–relation–object triple is not already present. This step adds relations without adding entities. It does not guarantee that coverage reaches the threshold. 

Entity resolution (across papers). Entities denoting the same concept can appear as distinct nodes. Once all papers are inserted, we lowercase their names, trim surrounding whitespace and remove punctuation. Nodes with matching normalized names and the same entity type are merged. This resolves surface-form variants but can leave synonyms with different names as separate nodes. Neither step introduces new entities: relation repair changes connectivity, while entity resolution consolidates existing nodes. These operations do not independently verify the scientific correctness of the extracted entities or relations.

Human reference. We estimate agreement between reviewers by split-half resampling. For each of 2,000 random splits, with seed 2026, we shuffle each idea’s ratings and assign ⌊k/ 2⌋ of its k

ratings to the first half and the remainder to the second. We average ratings within each half and compute the Spearman correlation between the two vectors of idea-level scores. This is an extrapolated reliability reference rather than observed agreement between two independent full panels. Its interpretation is approximate because the halves can be unequal, the number of ratings varies across ideas, and the adjustment is applied to Spearman correlations.

# J LIMITATIONS 

Literature and extraction. CAR builds its graph from papers whose full text it can obtain. In our experiments, some papers that could not be downloaded automatically were retrieved manually through institutional subscriptions. Deployments without equivalent access may construct different or less extensive corpora. Entities and relations are extracted by an LLM, and we do not measure extraction accuracy against expert annotation. Relation repair addresses missing connectivity but does not verify scientific correctness. Name-based merging can leave semantically overlapping entities separate. 

Cost. Graph construction and iterative ideation introduce additional calls and token usage. Recorded generation costs vary by backbone and method (Appendix I). Building the graph once per topic amortizes construction across ideas, but not the per-idea reasoning and retrieval costs. The reported estimates exclude evaluation and unrecorded failed attempts, so they are not complete end-to-end costs. 

Operators and auditability. The ablations do not establish a consistent quality benefit from explicit operators beyond free-form iteration. Some operator and validation contrasts are significant on one backbone, so these results should not be interpreted as showing that the components have no effect. The trace records tool interactions, accepted and rejected operations, and declared literature entities. Its guarantees are procedural and structural. An entity appearing in a tool result does not establish 27 Preprint. Under review. that the agent used it meaningfully, and an accepted operation need not be scientifically sound. The trace does not establish that each entity or operation was causally necessary to the proposal. 

Evaluation. Idea quality is assessed by LLM judges, without direct human evaluation of the gener-ated ideas’ quality. Agreement with expert ratings is assessed on a separate sample of NLP propos-als. This sample also informed development of the methodological novelty evaluator, so its reported agreement is not independent held-out validation. The adjusted split-half human-agreement estimate is a reference, not a second observed expert panel, and the comparison does not establish equiva-lence to human reviewers. Retrieval-based novelty is conditional on the evidence found and cannot establish novelty across the wider literature. The 40 -keyword sample limits precision, especially for domain analyses with ten keywords per do-main. Results differ between backbones, and nonsignificant contrasts do not establish the absence of an effect. Missing generations and incomplete MN panels yield available-case comparisons whose exclusions may be selective (Appendix E.1). Execution evaluates CAR’s proposals through one ex-ecutor on machine learning tasks, with a single run per task on BioXArena. Performance depends on the executor’s implementation and the task interface as well as the proposal. These experiments do not establish effectiveness across scientific research more broadly.
