## Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly being evaluated for autonomous driving through question-answering benchmarks, yet high benchmark accuracy does not necessarily reflect visually grounded reasoning. We present a diagnostic evaluation framework that systematically probes whether VLMs base safety-critical driving decisions on visual evidence or instead rely on language priors, answer biases, and dataset regularities. Our benchmark comprises two task categories, Adverse Weather and Junctions \& Intersections, with a nuScenes spatial reasoning extension, and evaluates open-source (general, driving, and spatial-reasoning) VLMs along four diagnostic protocols: image versus no-image evaluation, counterfactual weather sensitivity, linguistic robustness under paraphrasing and negation, and spatial reasoning. All ground-truth labels are derived programmatically from BDD100K and DriveLM annotations, enabling reproducible evaluation without manual labeling. Across all models, we find that competitive overall accuracy conceals systematic failure modes, including strong affirmation bias, limited sensitivity to counterfactual scene changes, near-chance performance on negated questions despite high paraphrase consistency, and confident driving decisions even without visual input. These results demonstrate that current open-source VLMs remain insufficiently grounded for safety-critical autonomous driving and underscore the need for diagnostic evaluation beyond aggregate accuracy.
