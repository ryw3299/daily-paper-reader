Abstract 

Large language models (LLMs) can answer many factual questions correctly, but a correct answer is not necessarily a supported one. For an LLM agent, a knowledge graph (KG) plays two roles: as a source of answers, it supplies facts that the model lacks; as a source of ev-idence, it supplies the supporting triples that make an answer traceable and auditable. How-ever, existing KG agents treat the KG only as a source of answers: their training data car-ries answers but not evidence, and their train-ing rewards check the answer but not the re-trieved evidence, so agents often answer cor-rectly without retrieving the supporting triples. We propose FoG , a framework that trains KG agents to retrieve the evidence behind their an-swers. FoG consists of evidence-grounded syn-thesis, a KG interaction agent, and an evidence-coverage process reward (ECPR): the synthesis recursively hides explicit entities behind ver-ifiable sub-questions, so that every question carries its supporting triples by construction; the agent records the triples returned by the KG; and ECPR credits each supporting triple to the turn that first retrieves it. Experiments on three KGQA benchmarks demonstrate that FoG enables 3B–4B open-source agents to out-perform substantially larger baselines in answer accuracy and evidence coverage, with zero-shot cross-KG transfer.

An LLM with no KG access can already answer many benchmark questions cor-rectly, and even an RL-trained agent can answer correctly while retrieving only part of the support-ing triples. We identify two causes. First, train-ing data carries answers but not evidence : most KGQA datasets annotate answers and at most log-ical forms (Talmor and Berant, 2018; Yih et al., 2016; Gu et al., 2021), and recovering the sup-porting triples afterwards requires enumerating and verifying paths for every question. Second, train-ing rewards check the answer but not the evidence :most rewards score only the final answer or whether the KG returns it (Lin et al., 2025; Song et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025), so a trajectory that reaches the answer through part of the evidence earns as much as one that retrieves all of it. The process rewards that go further depend on an LLM judge or on gold paths recovered for every question (Wang and Yu, 2026a; Yan et al., 2026), both of which are costly. Both causes trace back to one bottleneck: evidence is expensive to recover once a question has been written .To remove this bottleneck, we propose FoG (Fig-ure 1), a framework that trains KG agents to re-trieve the evidence behind their answers. Our key observation is that evidence is hard to recover after a question is written but can be embedded when the question is constructed and then rewarded dur-ing training. First, evidence-grounded synthesis 

recursively hides the explicit entities of a seed ques-tion behind verifiable sub-questions, so every ques-tion carries its supporting triples. Second, a KG interaction agent answers through multi-turn KG queries and records every triple that the KG returns. Lastly, during RL training, an evidence-coverage process reward (ECPR) gives a dense reward to each turn that first retrieves one of these supporting triples. Our contributions are as follows: • We distinguish two roles of the KG and show that existing training data and rewards serve it only as a source of answers, not of evidence. • We propose FoG, which embeds supporting triples into synthetic questions and rewards their retrieval through ECPR, so the dense reward comes from data synthesis with no LLM judge or extra annotation. • On three benchmarks, 3B–4B FoG agents outper-form larger baselines in accuracy and evidence coverage, and transfer zero-shot across KGs. 22 Related Work 

Knowledge graph augmented reasoning. Un-like prompting strategies (Wei et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023a; Jin et al., 2024), KG-augmented methods ground reasoning in verifiable triples and follow two paradigms. Retrieval-based methods (Luo et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2025; Mavromatis and Karypis, 2025; He et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2026) condition the LLM on triples, paths, or subgraphs retrieved in a single step. Agent-based methods (Sun et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025) explore the KG iteratively: ToG and PoG prompt a frozen LLM, whereas recent work trains the agent with RL (Lin et al., 2025; Song et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Wang and Yu, 2026b; Sun et al., 2026a). However, these agents use the KG only as a source of answers and can answer correctly without retrieving the supporting triples. In this work, we train agents to use it as a source of evidence. 

Synthetic data for KGQA. Synthetic data frees KGQA from human annotation, since questions can be generated from the KG and verified by exe-cution. It provides questions for agent training (Xu et al., 2026) and for benchmarking and retriever training (Zhang et al., 2025; Cattaneo et al., 2026). However, these pipelines treat it as extra examples to train or evaluate on. In this work, we treat it as a source of supervision: every synthesized question carries the evidence that defines its reward. 

Rewards for KG agents. RL lets a KG agent learn its retrieval policy from interaction with the KG. Most work trains from outcome rewards on the final answer with rule-based terms for valid interaction (Lin et al., 2025; Song et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Wang and Yu, 2026b; Sun et al., 2026a; Sun and Kazakov, 2026). Because outcome rewards are sparse, later work adds dense process rewards, obtained from an LLM judge (Wang and Yu, 2026a) or from gold paths and logical forms (Yan et al., 2026; Han et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2026b). However, both sources are costly: a judge must score every rollout, and gold references must be annotated or recovered for every question. In this work, the process reward comes from data synthesis: every synthesized ques-tion already carries its supporting triples, which provide a dense reward at no extra cost.

3.2 KG Interaction Agent 

To make the evidence retrieved for an answer ex-plicit, we develop a KG interaction agent that solves each question through a multi-turn think– act–observe loop with the action space 

A = {think , kg-query , answer }, (4) where think performs internal reasoning,

The kg-query action is instantiated with seven atomic operations that probe the relations of an entity, expand to its 4neighboring entities, filter an entity set, and combine entity sets (Appendix D). Every trajectory thus records the returned triples T (τ ), the only evidence that FoG rewards and evaluates.

4.2 Main Results (RQ1) 

As shown in Table 1, we compare FoG with all baselines in answer accuracy. 

Comparison across methods. FoG achieves the best F1 and Hit@1 on all three benchmarks, exceed-ing the strongest baseline KG-R1 by up to +3.73 F1 and +3.92 Hit@1. As KG-R1 is also RL-trained on the same Qwen3-4B backbone, this indicates that training an agent to retrieve evidence also makes its answers more accurate. 

Comparison across backbones. FoG-3B still ex-ceeds the 4B KG-R1 on all three benchmarks, and FoG-4B surpasses GPT-4o-mini agents by at least +7.58 F1 and 7B retrieval-based methods by at least +4.39 F1. This suggests that how an agent is trained to retrieve matters more than its backbone size.

5 Conclusion 

In this work, we present FoG, a framework that trains KG agents to retrieve the evidence behind their answers. By introducing evidence-grounded synthesis, a KG interaction agent, and an evidence-coverage process reward, FoG uses the KG as a source of evidence rather than only as a source of answers. Experiments on three KGQA benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements in answer ac-curacy, evidence coverage, and cross-KG transfer. 8Limitations 

KG completeness and freshness. FoG uses Wikidata triples as verifiable references for evidence-covered supervision, but real-world knowledge graphs may be incomplete, outdated, or noisy. When the required evidence is absent or incorrect in the graph, the agent may still fail despite following the intended evidence path. A natural extension is to equip the agent with a mech-anism for detecting insufficient on-graph evidence and invoking external retrieval when needed. 

Dependence on teacher models. Like other KG agents distilled from teacher trajectories (Chen et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2026a), FoG currently relies on an external teacher LLM (Kimi-K2) for ques-tion synthesis and trajectory distillation. Although symbolic checks filter invalid questions and evi-dence paths, the synthesis process still depends on the generation quality of external teachers. One promising direction is to replace teacher-driven synthesis with self-play, where agents generate, ex-plore, and verify candidate questions through graph execution and symbolic filtering. 

Long-horizon graph interaction. FoG caps graph interactions at 10 turns, which is sufficient for the 1–4-hop questions studied in our bench-marks. More open-ended KG reasoning may re-quire longer evidence chains and broader graph ex-ploration. Extending FoG to such settings requires balancing search depth against error accumulation, noisy observations, and sparser rewards. 

Ethical Considerations 

All experiments use publicly available KGQA datasets derived from Wikidata (CC0), QALD10-en (MIT License), and WikiWebQuestions, GT-SQA, and WebQSP (publicly available for re-search). Backbone models Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 are released under Apache 2.0; GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-V3, and the teacher Kimi-K2 are ac-cessed via official APIs under their terms of service. Our use of all existing artifacts is consistent with their intended research purposes. The datasets con-tain only public encyclopedic facts from structured KG triples, with no private or user-generated data. The only human involvement is the audit of con-structed evidence paths (Appendix F.3), in which three graduate annotators judged whether a KG path supports its question. The annotators took part voluntarily, the task exposed them to no sensitive content, and no personal data was collected. This work involves no crowdsourcing and no user study. FoG is intended to make the answers of KG agents traceable to retrieved evidence. Since the KG may be outdated or incorrect, this evidence supports hu-man verification rather than replaces it. We foresee no dual-use concerns.

Redundancy Check. To provide a clear overview of the non-redundancy check in Section 3.1, we present its procedure in the form of pseudocode. As shown in Algorithm 1, we first build a support graph from the supporting triples and extract the shortest path from each topic entity to the answer node. We then enumerate proper subsets of topic entities in increasing size and execute one SPARQL query per subset. If any subset yields an answer set no larger than that of the full query, the question admits a shortcut and is discarded. 

Complexity Analysis. Given a question with |Eq|

topic entities and |S (n)| supporting triples, extract-ing the shortest paths by BFS takes O(|Eq| · |S (n)|)

time. The subset enumeration issues at most 

2|Eq | − 2 SPARQL queries and stops at the first redundant subset. Using only the shortest path per topic entity is an approximation, which we audit on 200 candidate questions against an exhaustive enumeration of all path combinations under the same hop limit. The shortest-path check misses a shortcut in 1.5% of the candidates, while issuing 5.1 rather than 48.6 SPARQL queries per question. In summary, the redundancy check is dominated by SPARQL execution, and the shortest-path ap-proximation reduces the number of queries by 9.5 ×

F.2 Gold Supporting Triples 

The gold supporting triples used for evaluation are distinct from those produced by synthesis, which serve only as ECPR supervision during training. GTSQA provides gold answer subgraphs natively. For WikiWebQuestions, which has no such anno-tation, we follow EoG (Yan et al., 2026) and con-struct them in two stages. We first enumerate can-didate paths from each topic entity to each answer entity by BFS under a maximum-hop constraint. An LLM verifier then judges whether each candi-date path supports all constraints of the question, and the union of triples on the accepted paths forms the gold supporting triples.

G Baseline Details 

We compare FoG against nine representative base-lines that cover the LLM reasoning, retrieval-based, and agent-based paradigms: 

IO Prompt (OpenAI, 2023; Team, 2025b; DeepSeek-AI, 2024) is a KG-free baseline where the LLM directly answers the question from its parametric knowledge. This serves as a lower bound for KG-augmented reasoning. 

CoT (Wei et al., 2022) elicits step-by-step rea-soning before the final answer, still without any access to the KG. 

Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) samples multiple CoT chains and aggregates their answers by majority voting. 

RoG (Luo et al., 2024) generates relation paths as plans, retrieves reasoning paths along them, and answers from the retrieved paths. 

SubGraphRAG (Li et al., 2025) retrieves a sub-graph with a lightweight MLP that scores triples in parallel, and feeds it to an LLM reader. 

GCR (Luo et al., 2025) encodes KG paths into a Trie index and constrains LLM decoding with the graph structure. 

ToG (Sun et al., 2024) is a training-free agent that performs beam search over entities and rela-tions through multi-turn KG interaction. 

PoG (Chen et al., 2024) decomposes the ques-tion into sub-objectives and explores paths with memory updating and self-reflection. 

KG-R1 (Lin et al., 2025) trains a single KG agent end-to-end with GRPO under outcome and turn-level action-validity rewards.

I Case Study 

To better understand how much of the supporting evidence different methods retrieve from the KG, we present a case study on the question: “Which airline flies the Embraer 195 and is based at the airport named after Margaret Kenyatta’s father?” 

As shown in Tables 14, 15, and 16, this question requires resolving the father, the airport named after him, and the airline that is based there and flies the aircraft. All three methods predict the correct answer, but they differ in how much of the evidence chain they recover. 

FoG recovers the complete gold evidence. It issues nine KG turns and grounds each hop in the triples returned by the previous one, so every link of the chain can be read from the trajectory. In contrast, ToG performs four rounds of beam search but recovers only one relevant triple, missing the links for airport naming and airline hub, so its answer relies mainly on parametric knowledge. 

PoG answers after a single retrieval step, leaving an evidence chain that cannot be audited from the trajectory. This case highlights the strength of FoG in reaching the answer through evidence that is fully retrieved from the KG.

Core Concept Definitions 

Three Entity Roles in this task:

Triple Selection Principles 

• Subset selection. Select a subset of triples from the given set as the evidence basis. Prefer strong constraints that lead to clear, unambiguous answers. • Avoid redundancy. If a triple already infers B from A, do not select another path that also infers B (no shortcuts allowed). • Necessity. Question Design Principles 

• Information sources. All information must come exclusively from the selected triples. The question may adopt single-hop reasoning, multi-hop reasoning, intersection queries, union queries, counting, or reverse reasoning. • Answer requirements. The answer must be derived entirely from selected triples and directly verifiable via a SPARQL query. • Path guidelines. A Path is an explanation of the reasoning process, expressed as a reasoning chain describing how the answer is derived.
