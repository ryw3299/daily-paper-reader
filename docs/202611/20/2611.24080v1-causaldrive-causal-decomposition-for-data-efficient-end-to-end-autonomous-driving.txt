# Abstract 

Efficient use of expert demonstrations is essential for end-to-end autonomous driving. However, directly imitating ex-pert trajectories couples nominal intention, which speci-fies where the vehicle should go, with multimodal driving styles, which determine how that intention is executed. Dif-ferent combinations of these factors can yield similar expert trajectories, obscuring their respective causal contributions and forcing the policy to learn both from a single entangled target. This ambiguity limits how effectively the policy can exploit expert demonstrations. We propose CausalDrive , a causal decomposition framework for data-efficient driving policy learning that separates deterministic nominal in-tention prediction from probabilistic driving style model-ing . Specifically, we construct counterfactual nominal tra-jectories from navigation command, map topology, and ego state by removing style dependent effects, providing a Nom-inal Intention Planner with supervision independent of ex-pert trajectories. Conditioned on the predicted nominal intention and surrounding agents, a diffusion-based Style Execution Policy models diverse driving styles as a multi-modal distribution over residuals between nominal and ex-pert trajectories. This causally structured supervision re-duces redundant learning of shared nominal behavior in routine scenarios, directing expert supervision toward in-formative execution variations and improving data utiliza-tion. CausalDrive achieves 91.2 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 

89.5 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, matching or surpassing state-of-the-art performance. Further experiments demonstrate strong performance with limited expert data and clear ad-vantages in challenging interactive scenarios. 

# 1. Introduction 

End-to-end autonomous driving maps scene observations and driving context to future ego trajectories. Early stud-ies established imitation learning for driving policy learn-ing [1, 4, 6, 31, 41], while recent approaches introduced structured scene representations and planning-oriented ar-chitectures [12, 13, 16, 17, 22, 34, 36, 43, 46]. Despite these advances, most methods treat expert trajectories as monolithic supervision targets, entangling factors with dis-tinct causal roles. Learning these factors jointly obscures 1arXiv:2611.24080v1 [cs.CV] 18 Nov 2026 their respective contributions and limits the effective use of expert demonstrations. We distinguish two factors underlying driving behav-ior: a nominal intention that specifies where the vehicle should go, and multimodal driving styles that determine how that intention is executed. Given the navigation com-mand, map topology, and ego state, the nominal intention is represented by an intention-consistent trajectory [29, 32]. Driving styles, in contrast, describe how the ego vehicle re-sponds to surrounding agents, such as by following, yield-ing, or overtaking, while pursuing that intention. Different combinations of these factors can yield similar expert tra-jectories over a short planning horizon. As illustrated in Figure 1, a vehicle intending to turn left and one intend-ing to proceed straight with an aggressive execution style can exhibit similar lane-changing trajectories. The observed trajectory alone therefore provides no explicit signal for at-tributing the behavior to nominal intention or driving style. Directly imitating this trajectory requires the policy to infer both factors from a single entangled target, making their re-spective contributions ambiguous and reducing the sample efficiency of learning either factor. Existing approaches do not explicitly resolve this ambi-guity. Conventional planners based on regression directly predict the complete expert trajectory, coupling nominal intention and driving style within a single target. Gen-erative approaches [21, 25, 40, 44, 47, 48, 50] instead model a multimodal distribution over complete trajecto-ries, but typically leave the underlying sources of multi-modality entangled. Meanwhile, methods based on causal-ity [14, 26, 27, 30, 37, 49] mitigate spurious correlations at the representation or training level. Our approach instead explicitly decomposes the supervision target, assigning de-terministic nominal intention and probabilistic driving style to dedicated learning objectives. To this end, we introduce CausalDrive , a causal decom-position framework for data-efficient driving policy learn-ing . First, we construct counterfactual nominal trajectories from navigation commands, map topology, and ego state by removing style dependent effects. Each trajectory repre-sents how the ego vehicle would proceed under the speci-fied intention in the absence of surrounding agents. These targets supervise a Nominal Intention Planner (NIP) in-dependently of expert trajectories, and the NIP predicts a deterministic nominal trajectory. Second, conditioned on the predicted nominal trajectory and surrounding agents, a diffusion-based Style Execution Policy (SEP) models di-verse driving styles as a multimodal distribution over resid-uals between nominal and expert trajectories. This decomposition provides causally structured super-vision by aligning separate learning objectives with the dis-tinct roles of nominal intention and driving style. In rou-tine scenes, nominal trajectories already approximate ex-pert execution. Residual modeling therefore reduces redun-dant learning of nominal behavior and focuses expert su-pervision on informative style variations. Moreover, large residuals identify demonstrations that contribute substan-tial execution adjustments beyond the nominal reference, which improves both data utilization and challenging sce-nario mining. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness of CausalDrive. It achieves 91.2 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 

89.5 EPDMS on the more challenging NAVSIM v2, match-ing or surpassing state-of-the-art performance. Residual guided data selection yields strong performance with only 20% of the expert demonstrations, while further analyses show clear advantages in large residual interactive scenarios and more structured driving style distributions. Our contri-butions are summarized as follows: • We identify a causal ambiguity in direct imitation of ex-pert trajectories: different combinations of nominal inten-tion and driving style can yield similar trajectories, ob-scuring their respective contributions and limiting the ef-fective use of expert demonstrations. • We propose CausalDrive , a causally structured supervi-sion framework that separates deterministic nominal in-tention prediction from probabilistic driving style mod-eling. A Nominal Intention Planner learns from inde-pendent counterfactual targets, while a diffusion-based 

Style Execution Policy models execution residuals, re-ducing redundant learning of nominal behavior and en-abling residual guided mining of informative scenarios. • CausalDrive achieves 91.2 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 89.5 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2. Extensive experiments demonstrate strong data efficiency with residual guided data mining and clear advantages in large residual inter-active scenarios. 

# 2. Related Work 

2.1. End-to-End Autonomous Driving 

End-to-end autonomous driving maps sensor observations and driving context to future ego trajectories, enabling per-ception, prediction, and planning to be optimized toward a unified objective. Early approaches established imita-tion learning as an effective paradigm for driving policy learning and improved robustness through synthesized per-turbations or privileged supervision [1, 4, 15, 18, 28, 38, 39]. Subsequent work introduced structured scene repre-sentations and planning-oriented architectures. ST-P3 [12] learns spatial-temporal features for vision-based planning, UniAD [13] organizes perception and prediction around a planning objective, VAD [17] represents map elements and agent motion as vectors, and SparseDrive [36] develops a sparse, object-centric driving framework. Complementary causality-aware approaches [24, 26, 37] address spurious 2correlations through causal intervention or structured scene modeling. These advances improve scene modeling and policy learning, but generally retain the complete expert tra-jectory as the supervision target without separately super-vising nominal intention and driving style. CausalDrive in-stead decomposes this target into independently supervised nominal intention and expert-derived style residuals, mak-ing their causal roles explicit and reducing the ambiguity that limits effective use of expert demonstrations. 

2.2. Generative and Diffusion-Based Planning 

Generative planners model uncertainty in driving by pro-ducing diverse trajectory hypotheses rather than a single de-terministic prediction. GenAD [48] jointly generates agent motion and ego plans through latent generative modeling, while DiffusionDrive [25] employs truncated diffusion for efficient multimodal planning. DiffusionDriveV2 [50] fur-ther incorporates reinforcement learning to improve trajec-tory quality while preserving behavioral diversity. These methods model multimodality over complete trajectories without explicitly separating variation in nominal intention from variation in its execution. ResAD [49] instead mod-els normalized residuals relative to an inertial reference, simplifying trajectory generation. However, residual pa-rameterization alone does not provide an independently su-pervised, intention-consistent reference or explicitly isolate driving style. In contrast, CausalDrive learns a determinis-tic nominal trajectory from style-free counterfactual targets and conditions diffusion on this trajectory to model mul-timodal style residuals. The distinction lies not merely in generating residuals, but in assigning nominal intention and driving style to separate supervision targets, reducing the burden of learning their entangled distribution from expert demonstrations.

3.3. Architecture 

CausalDrive encodes multiple view images and LiDAR point clouds with a TransFuser-style fusion backbone [5]. The NIP decoder combines map features with the ego con-dition through cross-attention to predict ˆτ N . The SEP addi-tionally incorporates agent features and conditions residual denoising on the nominal prediction. The sampled residuals are added to the shared nominal trajectory to form candidate plans, which are evaluated by a coarse-to-fine ranker. 

Nominal Intention Planner. The NIP predicts how the ego vehicle would proceed under the nominal intention, without directly imitating the style dependent expert trajec-tory. A cross-attention decoder fuses encoded map features with the navigation command and ego state to predict ˆτ N .The counterfactual target ¯τ N in Eq. 12 provides its super-vision. Agent features are excluded from the NIP decoder, preventing direct conditioning of the nominal prediction on surrounding agent representations. 

Style Execution Policy. The SEP models diverse execu-tions of a given nominal intention as a conditional distri-bution over style residuals. A scene may admit multiple valid interaction responses, such as following, yielding, or overtaking, while the expert trajectory records only one re-alization. Instead of modeling these responses jointly with nominal intention in a distribution over complete trajecto-ries, the SEP represents them as deviations from ˆτ N . For each training sample, the residual target is 

δ0 = τ gt − sg[ˆ τ N ], (13) where sg[ ·] stops gradients through the nominal prediction, preventing the SEP objective from directly changing the nominal target or conditioning trajectory. We use diffusion to capture the conditional multimodality of these residuals rather than regressing a single correction. Within candidate generation, explicit agent features enter through the SEP rather than the NIP. The SEP learns the conditional distribution of style resid-uals through diffusion. We evaluate on the NAVSIM v1 [8] and NAVSIM v2 [3] benchmarks using their official metrics, PDMS and EPDMS, respectively. NAVSIM provides a suite of real-world, planning-centric driving scenarios built upon Open-Scene [7], which is a compact version of the extensive nu-Plan [2] dataset. The dataset is split into navtrain (1,192 training scenes) and navtest (136 evaluation scenes).

4.3. Main Results 

Results on NAVSIM v1 and v2. As shown in Tables 1 and 2, CausalDrive achieves 91.2 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 89.5 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2 with a ResNet-34 back-bone, attaining the highest reported PDMS and EPDMS among the compared methods. On NAVSIM v1, it achieves the best DAC (98.1) and TTC (95.9); on NAVSIM v2, it achieves the best NC (98.6), TTC (98.1), DDC (99.5), and TL (99.9), together with the best DAC (97.9) among ResNet-34 methods. These results indicate strong collision avoidance and rule compliance, consistent with the com-plementary roles of the two components: the NIP provides an intention-consistent nominal trajectory, while the SEP models the interaction responses required to execute it in surrounding traffic.

These examples illus-trate the complementary roles of the NIP and SEP: the for-mer establishes where the vehicle should go, while the latter adapts its execution to surrounding traffic.

# 5. Conclusion 

We presented CausalDrive , a causal decomposition frame-work that separates deterministic nominal intention predic-tion from probabilistic driving style modeling. By assign-ing shared nominal behavior to an independently supervised planner, CausalDrive reduces redundant learning from rou-tine scenarios and focuses expert supervision on additional execution behaviors. The resulting residual representation 8also enables targeted mining of informative scenarios, prior-itizing demonstrations that require substantial adjustments beyond nominal motion. Experiments on NAVSIM v1 and v2 demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, strong data ef-ficiency, and clear advantages in large residual interactive scenarios.
