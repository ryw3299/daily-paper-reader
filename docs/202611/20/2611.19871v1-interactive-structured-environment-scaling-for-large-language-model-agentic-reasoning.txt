## ABSTRACT 

Reinforcement learning via environmental interaction has become a cornerstone post-training paradigm for evolving large language models into autonomous agents capable of multi-turn tool use and knowledge exploration. Because the training environment defines what the policy can observe and how its actions are verified, the environment’s structure directly bounds the capabilities an agent can acquire. Yet existing structured and retrieval environments predominantly rely on upstream pre-filtering—extracting a compact per-question subgraph around the gold answer or retrieving a fixed passage list before the policy acts. We show that such pre-filtering introduces a fundamental measurement confound: because per-question subgraphs are constructed to enclose ground-truth paths, they compress the candidate space by orders of magnitude and leak answer lo-cations through local topology, allowing a question-blind degree heuristic to sur-face gold answers far above chance. To restore path selection, candidate pruning, and optimal stopping as genuine policy decisions, we propose RISE (Inte Ract Ive 

Structured Environment), transitioning structured reinforcement learning from static in-context subgraphs to full-scale interactive databases along two coupled axes. First, Decision-Space Scaling exposes the unpruned knowledge graph through a five-stage graph-agnostic typed intent adapter under a bounded call budget and a multiplicative grounding reward gate. Second, Learning-Frontier Scaling overcomes the severe within-group variance collapse of full-graph ex-ploration under group-normalized objectives by uniting model-independent struc-tural tier progression with a self-evolving competence-frontier bandit. Across nineteen benchmarks and three model backbones, RISE substantially advances in-domain and unseen-schema knowledge-graph reasoning, transfers zero-shot to open-domain passage retrieval, and significantly enhances out-of-domain mathe-matical and logical deduction. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) with reinforcement learning (RL) and verifiable feed-back has driven a paradigm shift in post-training, eliciting deliberative capabilities such as hypothe-sis testing, self-reflection, backtracking, and multi-step planning (Jaech et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025; Kimi Team et al., 2025; Comanici et al., 2025). By optimizing policies directly against outcome ver-ifiers, RL enables models to internalize reasoning strategies that are difficult to instil through passive imitation alone (Shao et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2025b). While early breakthroughs centered on symbolic domains such as mathematics, logic, and code generation (Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025c), this paradigm is rapidly expanding to interactive agentic settings, empowering LLMs to query search engines, navigate databases, and orchestrate external tools over multi-turn horizons (Yao et al., 2023; Schick et al., 2023; Jin et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2025; Qian et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2026). Despite rapid progress in RL optimization algorithms (Schulman et al., 2017; Shao et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2025a; Yu et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025a), the foundational role of the training environ-ment itself has been comparatively overlooked (Fang et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2026b). In interactive RL, the environment governs what the policy observes, which actions are admissible, and when an episode terminates, directly bounding what decision-making capabilities a policy can acquire (see 1arXiv:2611.19871v1 [cs.AI] 15 Nov 2026 Preprint. Appendix A for a detailed review of related work). Existing environments face a persistent trade-off across these dimensions. Internalized-rule environments (e.g., mathematics and code) provide clean verifiability, but rely on costly expert annotations and do not train agents to gather missing evidence under partial observability (Hendrycks et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024). Conversely, externalized-rule game and embodied environments support multi-turn exploration (Carta et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2024; Wen et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025), yet the learned skills often overfit to specialized me-chanics and fail to transfer to general reasoning. Large-scale structured knowledge bases offer an ideal foundation to bridge this gap (Codd, 1970; Bollacker et al., 2008; Lacombe et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2026a;b): their expansive schemas support automated scaling, their multi-hop paths mirror compositional deduction, and their explicit facts enable exact rule-based verification. However, for a structured environment to genuinely incentivize transferable agentic search and rea-soning rather than localized reading comprehension, it must simultaneously satisfy three core criteria (formalized in Appendix B): (1) Open Decision Space , where sequential path selection, candidate pruning, and optimal stopping governance remain open decisions for the policy; (2) Tool-Agnostic Action Interface , where query formulation is decoupled from backend-specific query syntax so that learned search habits transfer across heterogeneous tools; and (3) Active Learning Frontier ,where the training schedule sustains non-zero policy-gradient variance as the decision space scales. Yet existing structured and retrieval environments—whether injecting serialized subgraphs into the prompt (Luo et al., 2024; He et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2026a;b), walking pre-extracted question-specific subgraphs (Sun et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026), or retrieving fixed top-k passages (Jin et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025a; Song et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025)—violate the first criterion by design: they pre-filter the candidate space before the policy takes a single step .Treating such pre-filtered contexts as search environments introduces a severe measurement con-found before it imposes any capability ceiling. We quantify this confound on the per-question We-bQSP subgraphs released by a widely used RL knowledge-graph agent (Lin et al., 2025), auditing them exactly as published (Appendix B). Three findings stand out. First, the candidate space is compressed by five orders of magnitude, with the median subgraph holding only 4,415 triples over 

1,638 entities ( 2.16 × 10 5 times smaller than full Freebase). Second, 94 .6% of gold answers reside within two hops of a topic entity ( 65 .5% within one hop), allowing naive breadth-first enumeration to substitute for selective planning. Third, and most critically, because each subgraph is extracted to enclose the ground-truth path, its local connectivity directly leaks the answer’s location: a question-blind heuristic that never reads the question and simply ranks nodes by in-subgraph degree surfaces a gold answer in the top-10 for 33 .27% of questions, against 0.19% for a uniform random baseline (Figure 1A). When pre-extracted topology already points at the answer and guarantees reachability by construction ( ρ ≡ 1), accuracy differences cannot be reliably attributed to a learned search policy. To transform reachability from a construction artifact into a learnable policy outcome, we transition the training environment from static pre-extracted subgraphs to full-scale interactive databases and propose RISE (Inte Ract Ive Structured Environment). As illustrated in Figure 1, RISE scales agen-tic RL along two coupled dimensions. First, Decision-Space Scaling (Figure 1B) exposes the un-pruned Freebase store ( 9.5 × 10 8 triples, where a model-free breadth-first probe establishes a reach-ability floor of ρBFS ≥ 0.5219 ) under a finite call budget ( Cmax = 16 ) and a rolling context window (28 ,672 tokens), restoring candidate pruning and stopping control as authentic, turn-attributable pol-icy decisions. Rather than binding actions to raw SPARQL syntax (Chen et al., 2025d; Song et al., 2026) or schema-specific primitives (Lin et al., 2025; Gong et al., 2026), RISE interposes a deter-ministic five-stage graph-agnostic typed intent adapter ΦG that executes natural-language queries symmetrically across outgoing and incoming edges ( 48 .6% of candidate relations exist solely as in-coming edges), paired with a multiplicative grounding reward gate and an isomorphic protocol cold start (Figure 2). Because the policy interacts strictly through natural-language intents and structured receipts, it transfers zero-shot to unseen schemas, temporal knowledge graphs (Chen et al., 2023; Gong et al., 2026), and unstructured passage retrievers (Yan et al., 2026), evaluated unconditionally on full official splits. Second, Learning-Frontier Scaling (Figure 1C) addresses the optimization bottleneck that decision-space enlargement exposes: within-group variance collapse under group-normalized RL (Shao et al., 2024). Learning to search is learning a sequence of decisions, not learning a query format. An environment carries that signal only if sequential path selection, candidate pruning, and stopping governance are genuinely open: once candidates are pre-enumerated and the answer enclosed upstream, the only task left is local evidence stitching. Section 2.1 defines the sequential decisions a search policy must make, but if the action space di-rectly encodes a database’s native query syntax (such as SPARQL), the decision space becomes bound to that schema and cannot transfer across tools. We therefore restrict the policy’s action vo-cabulary to natural language plus bracketed entity mentions, placing all schema-specific translation behind a deterministic adapter ΦG so that the exact same policy weights can drive a static knowledge graph, a temporal graph, or a text passage retriever without retraining. RISE implements this decoupling via a four-layer architecture: (1) a full-scale Virtuoso SPARQL backend hosting Freebase; (2) a deterministic five-stage typed intent adapter ΦG ; (3) a strict envi-ronment contract governing turn grammar, receipt schemas, and error codes; and (4) a trainer-facing multi-turn conversation interface shared identically between structured graph search and unstruc-tured passage retrieval. Because the top conversation layer is invariant across backends, swapping the adapter is the only change required to evaluate a graph-trained policy on a passage retriever. 73% zero-variance rate observed at G = 4 ): mastered questions ( pq → 1) and questions that are unreachable or currently beyond the policy’s horizon ( pq → 0) both collapse to ˆσ = 0 . Because ˆσ(q)

is a per-question property, a batch mixing half pq = 1 and half pq = 0 questions still produces zero gradient despite a mean pass rate of 0.5. Sustaining active gradients therefore requires aligning both the macro progression direction and the micro batch composition Psched with the policy’s evolving competence frontier ( pq ≈ 0.5). 

Model-Independent Structural Tiers and Macro Frontier Direction. To partition QRL with-out circular model-based perplexity, we assign each question a deterministic structural tier d(q) ∈{1, 2, 3, 4} parsed directly from its gold SPARQL topology and official structural annotations: Tier 1 comprises single-hop relation lookups and simple chains, Tier 2 comprises multi-hop 

composition chains, Tier 3 comprises multi-constraint conjunction intersections, and Tier 4 comprises comparative and superlative aggregation queries. At the start of RL, the pol-icy’s competence frontier ( pq ≈ 0.5) resides in simpler lookups and short chains while harder multi-constraint tiers sit near pq ≈ 0. Presenting tiers in progressive order ( d = 1 → 2 → 3 → 4,

Easy-to-Hard , E2H) provides an open-loop approximation to the expanding frontier, whereas re-versing the exact same 28 ,215 -question multiset ( d = 4 → 3 → 2 → 1, Hard-to-Easy , H2E) front-loads zero-variance hard tiers when the policy cannot solve them and defers easy tiers until they are already mastered.

## 3 EXPERIMENTS 

To systematically evaluate RISE and dissect the contribution of each component in its training recipe, we conduct comprehensive experiments across nineteen benchmarks spanning three capa-bility families to answer four research questions (RQs): (1) RQ1 : Can effective multi-hop search policies be learned directly over an unpruned, full-scale structured environment, and how well do they generalize to unseen schemas and across model scales? (2) RQ2 : Can a search policy trained exclusively on a structured knowledge graph transfer zero-shot to unstructured open-domain passage retrieval? (3) RQ3 : Do the hypothesis-testing, pruning, and verification habits acquired through in-teractive structured exploration generalize out-of-domain to enhance tool-free mathematical and log-ical reasoning? (4) RQ4 : How do the protocol-preserving cold start, progressive frontier ordering, and online variance-driven batch composition (SEC) distinctively contribute to agentic learning? 3.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

3.1.1 DATASETS AND METRICS 

We evaluate on nineteen benchmarks under greedy decoding ( T = 0 ), grouped into three capabil-ity families: (1) structured knowledge (6 sets)—WebQSP (Yih et al., 2016) and CWQ (Talmor & Berant, 2018) in-domain, GrailQA (Gu et al., 2021), SimpleQuestions (Bordes et al., 2015), and WebQuestions (Berant et al., 2013) for unseen-schema transfer, and MultiTQ (Chen et al., 2023) for temporal reasoning; (2) cross-tool passage retrieval (7 sets)—the single-fact sets NQ (Kwiatkowski et al., 2019), TriviaQA (Joshi et al., 2017), and PopQA (Mallen et al., 2013), and the multi-hop sets HotpotQA (Yang et al., 2018), 2WikiMultiHopQA (Ho et al., 2020), MuSiQue (Trivedi et al., 2022), and Bamboogle (Press et al., 2023); and (3) tool-free general reasoning (6 sets)—GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021), MATH500 (Hendrycks et al., 2021), AIME24, AMC23, and Knights and Knaves Easy and Hard (Xie et al., 2024; 2025). Metrics follow standard conventions per family and are never pooled across families: set-equality EM for WebQSP, CWQ, and GrailQA; any-item hit ( †) for Sim-pleQuestions and WebQuestions; Hits@1 for MultiTQ; alias EM for passage retrieval; and exact accuracy for general reasoning, with Avg. reporting the within-table mean. 3.1.2 BASELINES

## 4 CONCLUSION 

What defines an autonomous retrieval agent is the sequence of decisions it makes: which path to explore, which candidates to prune, and when to stop searching. All three decisions vanish when the training environment pre-extracts a local subgraph or lets an upstream index decide what is reach-able in advance. RISE transitions structured RL environments to full-scale interactive databases along two coupled axes: Decision-Space Scaling exposes the unpruned knowledge graph through a five-stage graph-agnostic typed intent adapter under a bounded call budget, restoring pruning and stopping as turn-attributable policy decisions; and Learning-Frontier Scaling overcomes the 64% 

within-group variance collapse of full-graph exploration by aligning progressive tier ordering and closed-loop SEC batch composition with the policy’s evolving competence frontier. Across nine-teen benchmarks and three model backbones, RISE substantially outperforms specialized baselines in structured KG reasoning, transfers zero-shot to Wikipedia passage retrieval, and generalizes out-of-domain to enhance tool-free mathematical and logical deduction. 12 Preprint. This work trains and evaluates retrieval-augmented language models exclusively over publicly re-leased benchmarks and knowledge bases (Freebase, MultiTQ, and Wikipedia), involving no human subjects or private user data. Knowledge-Graph Reasoning Agents. Prompt-driven frameworks traverse a graph by prompting a frozen model to select edges iteratively: Think-on-Graph (Sun et al., 2024) performs beam search over relations, and Plan-on-Graph (Chen et al., 2024) maintains a decomposed plan with reflection and adaptive beam width. A second line retrieves a question-specific subgraph prior to generation, as in Reasoning-on-Graphs (Luo et al., 2024) and G-Retriever (He et al., 2024). A third line trains the policy end to end via RL: KG-R1 (Lin et al., 2025) optimizes multi-turn retrieval with four symbolic one-hop primitives over pre-extracted per-question subgraphs; GraphWalker (Xu et al., 2026) pretrains on synthetic walking trajectories and applies RL over relation and triple lookups; KnowCoder-A1 (Chen et al., 2025d) emits executable SPARQL queries assisted by schema-lookup tools; ISP-KGR (Song et al., 2026) couples interactive semantic parsing with tree-structured roll-outs; and Temp-R1 (Gong et al., 2026) applies a reverse curriculum over temporal primitives. Two fundamental differences separate RISE from this line. First, regarding decision-space scale , a me-dian per-question subgraph in the KG-R1 release holds only 4,415 triples, enclosing the gold path in advance; RISE exposes the full 9.5×10 8-triple store under a bounded call budget. Notably, Xu et al. (2026) report that evaluating subgraph-trained KG-R1 on the global graph drops WebQSP accuracy from 82 .7% (in-subgraph) to 66 .8% , directly confirming the magnitude of the subgraph shortcut. Second, regarding interface abstraction , SPARQL generation and graph-specific primitives bind the policy to one database schema, whereas our natural-language intent adapter transfers zero-shot to unstructured passage retrieval. 

Reinforcement Learning for Search and Tool Use. Interleaving chain-of-thought reasoning with external tool calls (Yao et al., 2023; Schick et al., 2023) via outcome-rewarded RL has been widely studied on text corpora: Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025) formulates multi-turn passage retrieval with observation-token masking, while ReSearch (Chen et al., 2025a), R1-Searcher (Song et al., 2025), WebThinker (Li et al., 2025), and DeepResearcher (Zheng et al., 2025b) extend outcome-driven RL to multi-step web and corpus search. ToolRL (Qian et al., 2025) studies reward granularity for general API invocation, SimpleTIR (Xue et al., 2026) stabilizes multi-turn tool-integrated training by filtering degenerate turns, Explore-on-Graph (Yan et al., 2026) introduces path-refined reward modeling, and ZeroSearch (Sun et al., 2025) replaces live search engines with a learned simulator whose noise is annealed over training. While effective on homogeneous text corpora where every query returns a stateless top-k list with smooth similarity decay, directly applying text-RL recipes to a full-scale structured knowledge base encounters ungrounded guessing on multi-answer queries and severe zero-variance gradient starvation—which RISE resolves via our multiplicative grounding gate g(τ ) and competence-frontier scheduling. 

Structured Post-Training Environments and Curriculum Learning. A parallel research line constructs post-training environments from structured data (Fang et al., 2025; Lacombe et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). KaLM (Yu et al., 2026a) aligns internal LLM representations with KG triples via dual-view contrastive learning, embedding structured knowledge into model parameters rather 19 Preprint. than context. SIE (Yu et al., 2026b) automatically extracts static in-context subgraphs of controlled completeness ( ρ ≡ 1 at SIE-100%) to incentivize compositional deduction via GRPO. ReMA (Wan et al., 2025) explores multi-agent meta-thinking under RL, Wen et al. (2024) study action decom-position for language agents, and Logic-RL (Xie et al., 2025) demonstrates reasoning transfer from rule-based logic puzzles. The key distinction lies in what is scaled: SIE scales the completeness of a static context that the model passively reads, whereas RISE scales the decision space of an interactive search environment that the model actively queries , where reachability is governed by the policy’s own sequential decisions under a call budget. On the optimization side, classical cur-riculum learning orders examples statically by difficulty (Bengio et al., 2009; Kumar et al., 2010), whereas prioritized replay and bandit schedulers adapt sampling online (Schaul et al., 2016; Auer et al., 2002). Building upon Chen et al. (2025b), we ground curriculum design in Proposition 1 by uniting SPARQL structural tiers with the group-level standard deviation return 2ppq (1 − pq ).

## B PRELIMINARY MEASUREMENTS AND ENVIRONMENT DESIGN 

## REQUIREMENTS 

This appendix provides the complete empirical protocols and measurements underlying the envi-ronment design of RISE. Specifically, we formalize three measurable criteria that an interactive en-vironment must satisfy to support authentic search-policy learning, evaluate existing subgraph and query-syntax paradigms against these criteria across four diagnostic measurement sets, and derive the six engineering and methodological requirements summarized in Table 6. B.1 THREE MEASURABLE CRITERIA FOR AGENTIC SEARCH ENVIRONMENTS 

Treating multi-turn retrieval as a learnable decision process requires an environment that simultane-ously satisfies three foundational conditions: 

(C1) Unavoidable Candidate Pruning. If the candidate space is small enough to enumerate within a few steps, sequential path selection requires no strategic pruning. Given any candidate environ-ment, we audit C1 by measuring: (a) the fraction of ground-truth answers reachable within one or two hops from the topic entity, and (b) the top-k answer hit rate achievable by a question-blind structural heuristic that never reads the input question. If (b) substantially exceeds uniform random chance, the environment’s topology leaks the answer’s location. 

(C2) Graph-Agnostic Action Interface. If the policy’s action space is bound to a database-specific query syntax (e.g., SPARQL or specialized atomic graph signatures), the policy conflates strategic search planning with syntax generation, obstructing zero-shot transfer to new graph schemas or unstructured text retrievers. C2 is satisfied when the policy emits natural-language search intents while a deterministic environment adapter handles schema-specific translation. 

(C3) Turn- and Outcome-Level Attributability. If the training reward incorporates intermediate process shaping (e.g., bonus credit whenever a gold entity appears anywhere in a retrieved table), score improvements cannot be cleanly attributed to improved decision-making versus shaping ex-ploitation. Attributability requires an outcome-only reward gated by explicit evidence grounding, paired with closed-vocabulary turn receipts. B.2 MEASUREMENT 1: P ER -Q UESTION SUBGRAPHS COMPRESS THE DECISION SPACE AND 

LEAK ANSWER LOCATIONS 

We audited the per-question WebQSP subgraphs released by KG-R1 (Lin et al., 2025) across all test instances without modification. Hop counts are computed via breadth-first search (BFS) as the shortest path length from any linked topic entity to any gold answer entity inside the extracted subgraph. To test for topological leakage, we evaluate a question-blind degree heuristic that ranks all nodes in the subgraph strictly by their in-subgraph degree (breaking ties deterministically by entity identifier) without accessing the question text, compared against a uniform random node baseline averaged over 1,000 Monte Carlo trials per question. Structured Receipt Schema. Each valid observation receipt contains four standardized fields: (1) Resolved , reporting the surface form and matched graph node identifier (allowing the policy to verify entity resolution without generating raw MIDs); (2) intent , recording the classified structural intent tag ( ιt ∈ { relation hop , relation list , entity info }); (3) Results (k of N) , listing the top-k ranked triples formatted as subject | relation | object

Closed-Vocabulary Error Codes. All execution anomalies are mapped deterministically to seven machine-readable error codes: • E NO ENTITY : Query contains no bracketed entity mention or no candidate exceeds the resolu-tion threshold. • E ENTITY NOT FOUND : Bracketed surface form cannot be resolved in the active binding table or graph index. • E INTENT UNSUPPORTED : Requested structural operation is inapplicable to the resolved node type. • E NO RESULTS : Well-formed query executed successfully but returned zero matching triples (N = 0 ). • E QUERY FAILED : Transient backend timeout or SPARQL engine exception; never penalized or counted against policy error streaks. • E BUDGET EXCEEDED : Search call cap Cmax = 16 reached; triggers the final answer-only turn. • E MALFORMED : Assistant output violated the turn grammar (<think> followed by 

<search> or <answer> ); permits retry on the next turn if budget remains. 

Dual-Budget Accounting and Observation Independence. The search call cap ( Cmax = 16 ), per-turn generation limit ( 2,048 tokens), and rolling context window ( 28 ,672 tokens, enforced via left truncation) operate orthogonal to one another. Cold-start trajectory filtering, online GRPO roll-outs, and benchmark evaluation invoke the exact same adapter binary and receipt serializer.

## I DISCUSSION : M ETHODOLOGICAL PRINCIPLES FOR AGENTIC RL 

Two broader methodological principles emerge from our study. First, reward modifications must be audited alongside answer-shape-invariant diagnostics : any change to the outcome verifier that relaxes set equality or removes grounding gating ( g(τ ), Section 2.3) can produce apparent gains on exact-match metrics simply by altering the length or enumeration behavior of the policy’s output. Enforcing multiplicative evidence grounding alongside strict set-equality verification is therefore essential for separating genuine search improvement from reward hacking. Second, curriculum scheduling in group-normalized RL is fundamentally policy-indexed : as estab-lished in Proposition 1, a question contributes non-zero gradient if and only if its rollout group exhibits positive reward variance ( ˆσ > 0). Because the competence frontier ( p(q; θ) ≈ 0.5) shifts continuously as the policy updates and varies across model capacities, reversing the difficulty order (H2E) starves early updates of gradient, whereas progressive alignment (E2H) and online bandit tracking (SEC) keep batches anchored where the policy can actively learn. 

## J LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

We highlight three limitations that suggest directions for future work. First, under a finite call bud-get ( Cmax = 16 ), questions whose surface forms fail entity resolution or require complex seman-tic bridging beyond dense relation ranking remain unreachable; integrating adaptive entity-linking tools or hybrid graph-text retrieval within the same episode is a promising extension. Second, our curriculum experiments hold the training horizon fixed at one epoch ( 440 steps) to ensure clean, horizon-free attribution across all arms; exploring multi-epoch competence-frontier scheduling on 29 Preprint. larger heterogeneous tool ecosystems remains open. Third, every comparison here uses a single training run per arm, so scaling multi-seed training across larger model families ( > 30 B parameters) will further strengthen variance estimates.
