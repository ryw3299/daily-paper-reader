# NaviGATE: Noise-Aware Dual-View Generation for Multimodal Knowledge Graph Reasoning 

Jie Peng, Yongxue Shan, Yongfu Zha, Cui Miao, Xiaodong Wang 

College of Computer Science and Technology National University of Defense Technology Changsha 410073, China {pengjie, shanyongxue001, zhayongfu, miaocui1024, xdwang}@nudt.edu.cn 

Abstract 

Multimodal knowledge graph reasoning (MKGR) aims to infer missing facts by jointly exploiting relational structures, tex-tual descriptions, and visual evidence. Existing embedding-based methods effectively capture graph topology, but their fixed multimodal representations are less effective for query-specific fine-grained semantic reasoning, while generation-based methods are vulnerable to noisy retrieval and may pro-duce invalid entities. To address these challenges, we propose NaviGATE, a noise-aware generative framework for MKGR. NaviGATE first aligns graph-derived structural representa-tions with textual semantics, then performs structure-aware internal knowledge activation by fine-tuning the multimodal language model with multimodal entity summaries and in-jected structural tokens. In the noise-aware dual-view instruc-tion tuning stage, NaviGATE jointly optimizes retrieval-free and retrieval-aware views: reliable retrieved candidates are reinforced through contrastive logit sharpening, while mis-leading candidates are suppressed through parametric consis-tency distillation. In inference, structure-constrained decod-ing restricts generation to valid entities in the target knowl-edge graph. Experiments on standard benchmarks show that NaviGATE consistently outperforms strong unimodal, mul-timodal, and generation-based baselines, and remains robust under noisy retrieval scenarios. 

Introduction 

Multimodal knowledge graphs (MMKGs) integrate struc-tured relational facts with multimodal information, such as textual descriptions and visual images, and have become important knowledge resources for artificial intelligence ap-plications, including recommendation systems (Xian et al. 2019; Kim et al. 2024), question answering (Li, Wang, and Zhu 2020), and information retrieval (Li, Ao, and He 2024; Kim et al. 2025). However, real-world knowledge graphs are often incomplete, with many valid facts missing from the observed graph. Multimodal knowledge graph reasoning (MKGR) aims to predict missing entities by jointly exploit-ing graph structure and entity-associated multimodal context, and has attracted increasing attention in recent years. Existing MKGR methods can be broadly categorized into embedding-based and generation-based approaches. Embedding-based methods encode entities, relations, and multimodal attributes into continuous representation spaces, and predict missing facts by scoring candidate triples. With graph neural networks (Li et al. 2023; Peng et al. 2025), translation-based constraints (Jian et al. 2025; Li et al. 2026), or multimodal fusion modules (Zhang et al. 2024a, 2025, 2024c), these methods effectively preserve structural topol-ogy and enrich entity representations with textual and visual evidence. However, their dependence on fixed multimodal representations and predefined scoring functions limits their ability to perform query-specific fine-grained semantic rea-soning. As a result, when multiple candidates are seman-tically similar, embedding-based models may narrow down the search space but remain insufficient to distinguish the exact target entity. Generation-based methods (Yang et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2024b) offer a promising alternative by formulating MKGR as a text generation problem. Leveraging the semantic under-standing and parametric knowledge of multimodal large lan-guage models (MLLMs), these methods can generate miss-ing entities from textualized queries and multimodal con-texts. Compared with embedding-based models, they pro-vide greater flexibility in contextual reasoning and can better exploit relation semantics, entity descriptions, and visual in-formation. Nevertheless, direct generation may produce an-swers that are not valid entities in the target knowledge graph or are inconsistent with the graph schema, which limits their completion performance. Recent retrieval-augmented frame-works (Liu et al. 2025b; Wei et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2025a) mitigate this issue by using an embedding-based retriever to obtain a top-K candidate set, which is then provided to the MLLM for reranking or generation. By combining structural recall with semantic reasoning, these methods have achieved notable performance improvements. However, this pipeline is fundamentally constrained by retriever recall: if the ground-truth entity is absent from the top-K candidate set, MLLMs may fail to reliably generate the correct answer; meanwhile, feeding the entire entity set into the model is impractical due to their limited context windows and attention budgets. In realistic MKGR scenarios, retrieved candidates are not always reliable, since the ground-truth entity may be absent from the candidate set, making the retrieved candidate set noisy. Over-reliance on retrieval may force the generator to select an incorrect entity from the candidate set. This raises a key challenge: how can a generative MKGR model rely on retrieved evidence when it is reliable, while falling back on the LLM’s internal knowledge when the retrieved arXiv:2611.24552v1 [cs.AI] 18 Nov 2026 candidate set is noisy? 

To address this challenge, we propose NaviGATE, a progressively optimized generative framework for MKGR. First, structure-semantic collaborative alignment coordinates structural entity representations with textual semantics in a shared space, enabling graph-derived knowledge to be integrated into the MLLM. Second, structure-aware inter-nal knowledge activation trains the model with multimodal entity summaries and structural cues under a retrieval-free view, preserving its basic completion ability when retrieval is noisy. Third, noise-aware dual-view instruction tuning jointly optimizes retrieval-free and retrieval-aware predic-tions: reliable candidates are strengthened through con-trastive logit sharpening, while misleading candidates are suppressed through parametric consistency distillation. Dur-ing inference, structure-constrained decoding restricts out-puts to valid KG entities. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows: 

• We propose structure-semantic collaborative alignment and internal knowledge activation to inject graph-derived knowledge into the MLLM, building retrieval-free com-pletion ability as a reliable fallback under noisy retrieval. 

• We introduce noise-aware dual-view instruction tuning, which strengthens reliable candidates via contrastive logit sharpening and suppresses misleading ones via paramet-ric consistency distillation. Structure-constrained decod-ing further ensures valid entity generation. 

• Experiments on MKG-W and DB15K show that Navi-GATE achieves the best MRR and Hits@1 among fifteen baselines and remains robust under noisy retrieval. 

Related Work 

Embedding-based knowledge graph reasoning 

Embedding-based knowledge graph reasoning (KGR) learns entity and relation representations and predicts missing facts by scoring candidate triples. Classical models, such as TransE (Bordes et al. 2013a), DistMult (Yang et al. 2015), ComplEx (Trouillon et al. 2016), RotatE (Sun et al. 2019), and TuckER (Balazevic et al. 2019), mainly exploit struc-tural triples to model relational patterns. MKGR methods further incorporate images and textual descriptions into en-tity representation learning, including IKRL (Xie et al. 2017), RSME (Wang et al. 2021), OTKGE (Cao et al. 2022), IMF (Li et al. 2023), QEB (Wang et al. 2023), VISTA (Lee et al. 2023), NativE (Zhang et al. 2024a), MyGO (Zhang et al. 2025), and APKGR (Jian et al. 2025). These methods im-prove structural retrieval through modality fusion, interac-tion, adaptive learning, or noise modeling, but still rely on fixed representations and predefined scoring functions. As fine-grained multimodal clues are compressed before infer-ence, their query-specific reasoning ability remains limited when candidates are semantically similar. 

Generation-based knowledge graph reasoning 

Generation-based KGR formulates link prediction as a text generation task, where a generative language model produces the missing entity from a textualized query. Early meth-ods, such as GenKGR (Xie et al. 2022) and KG-S2S (Yang et al. 2025), convert triples into natural language sequences and apply sequence-to-sequence models for entity gener-ation. With the emergence of large language models, re-cent studies further explore instruction tuning, in-context learning, and retrieval-augmented generation for KGR. For instance, KG-LLM (Shu et al. 2025) and KoPA (Zhang et al. 2024b) finetunes LLMs on KG completion instructions, while KICGPT (Wei et al. 2023), SAT (Liu et al. 2025a), and DIFT (Liu et al. 2025b) combine embedding-based retrieval with LLM-based reranking or finetuning. These methods demonstrate the potential of language models in leveraging semantic priors for KGR. Nevertheless, they often remain sensitive to retrieval quality. When retrieved candidates are noisy or the ground-truth entity is absent, generation models can be misled by irrelevant evidence. Moreover, directly gen-erated answers may not always correspond to valid entities in the KG. In contrast, our framework targets multimodal KGR by combining noise-aware dual-view instruction tuning with structure-constrained inference, achieving robust and valid entity generation under noisy multimodal retrieval.

As shown in Figure 1, NaviGATE progressively performs structure-semantic collaborative alignment, retrieval-free in-ternal knowledge activation, and noise-aware dual-view in-struction tuning to exploit reliable candidates while mitigat-ing misleading ones. Fin ally, structure-constrained decoding ensures valid entity generation.

Retrieval-augmented methods depend heavily on the quality of the retrieved candidate set. However, in realistic MKGR scenarios, the ground-truth entity may be absent due to lim-ited retriever recall. To reduce such over-dependence on re-trieval, NaviGATE first activates the model’s retrieval-free completion ability before exposing it to retrieved candidates. 

Query-aware multimodal entity summarization. Raw entity descriptions, images, and neighboring triples can be lengthy, redundant, and noisy. Directly concatenating them into the prompt may cause context dilution and make it dif-ficult for the MLLM to identify query-relevant evidence. Therefore, for each entity e involved in query q, we construct a compact query-aware multimodal summary: 

Sqe = Summarize ( q, d e, i e, Ne) , (9) where de is the textual description, ie is the associated image, and Ne denotes the local structural neighborhood of e. The summary Se converts heterogeneous evidence into a concise textual form, highlighting entity attributes, visual cues, and graph-neighborhood semantics relevant to query.

Experiments 

Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate NaviGATE on two widely used mul-timodal knowledge graph reasoning benchmarks, namely DB15K and MKG-W. DB15K contains 12,842 entities, 279 relations, 79,222 training triples, 9,902 validation triples, and 9,904 test triples. MKG-W contains 15,000 entities, 169 relations, 34,196 training triples, 4,276 validation triples, and 4,274 test triples. Both datasets provide structural triples together with multimodal entity information, making them suitable for evaluating the ability of MKGR models. 

Baselines. We compare NaviGATE with three categories of baseline methods. First, we include representative uni-modal knowledge graph embedding models that rely only on structural triples, including TransE (Bordes et al. 2013a), DistMult (Yang et al. 2015), ComplEx (Trouillon et al. 2016), RotatE (Sun et al. 2019), and TuckER (Balazevic et al. 2019). Second, we compare with discriminative MKGR methods that incorporate visual or textual information into entity rep-resentation learning, including OTKGE (Cao et al. 2022), IMF (Li et al. 2023), QEB (Wang et al. 2023), AdaMF (Zhang et al. 2024d), SnAg (Chen et al. 2025), NativE (Zhang et al. 2024a), MyGO (Zhang et al. 2025), and APKGR (Jian et al. 2025). Third, we include generation-based KGR methods, in-cluding DIFT (Liu et al. 2025b) and SAT (Liu et al. 2025a), to evaluate whether NaviGATE can leverage generative reason-ing while maintaining robustness to noisy retrieval results. 

Implementation Details. We conduct all experiments on an Intel Xeon Gold CPU with 4 NVIDIA 4090 GPUs, each with 24 GB memory, under Ubuntu 18.04 LTS. We select TuckER and RotatE as representative embedding-based re-trievers. Each retriever is first pretrained on the training set, and the top-20 predicted entities are then retrieved for each query as candidate entities. For the multimodal generative backbone, we use Qwen3-VL 8B as the base model and adopt LoRA (Hu et al. 2022) for parameter-efficient finetuning. The LoRA rank, scaling factor, and dropout rate are set to 64, 16, and 0.1, respectively. We train the model for 2 epochs in the internal knowledge activation stage and 4 epochs in the dual-view instruction tuning stage. Hyperparameter Sensitivity. Figure 3 shows the sensitiv-ity of NaviGATE to λkl and ε on MKG-W. For λkl , the model performs stably when it ranges from 0.01 to 0.5, while a larger value of 1.0 slightly degrades both MRR and Hits@1. This suggests that moderate parametric consistency helps suppress noisy retrieval, whereas overly strong regulariza-tion may limit the benefit of retrieval-aware evidence. For 

ε, the best performance is achieved around 0.2–0.5. A small 

ε provides insufficient logit sharpening, while a large value over-amplifies retrieval-induced shifts and introduces noise. Therefore, we set λkl = 0 .1 and ε = 0 .2 by default. 

Case Study. Figure 4 shows representative cases under different retrieval conditions. When the ground-truth entity appears in the candidate list, NaviGATE exploits retrieval evidence to generate the correct answer. More importantly, even when the ground-truth entity is absent from the candi-dates, NaviGATE can still recover it using activated internal knowledge. The failure cases further show that distinguish-ing highly confusable entities under ambiguous multimodal cues remains challenging, highlighting the inherent difficulty of noise-aware reasoning in MKGR. 

Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose NaviGATE, a noise-aware gen-erative framework for multimodal knowledge graph reason-ing. NaviGATE aligns structural representations with tex-tual semantics, activates retrieval-free completion through multimodal summaries and structural token injection, and employs dual-view instruction tuning to exploit reliable can-didates while suppressing misleading ones via parametric consistency. Structure-constrained decoding further ensures valid entity generation. Experiments demonstrate its effec-tiveness and robustness. In future work, we will extend Navi-GATE to incorporate more modality information beyond text and images, and explore robust designs for missing-modality scenarios to improve real-world MKGR reliability.
