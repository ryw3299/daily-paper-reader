## 摘要
注意力已成为估计周围物体相关性并解释自动驾驶车辆驾驶决策的最广泛采用的解决方案之一。然而，其捕捉单个输入元素对 AI 模型输出的因果影响的能力，长期以来在文献中受到讨论，尤其是在自然语言处理（NLP）领域。本工作将这一讨论引入自动驾驶领域，通过分析注意力在估计输入物体对自动驾驶车辆驾驶的影响（即其相关性）方面的准确性。为此，我们将不同的基于注意力的相关性估计方法与通过反事实分析获得的物体相关性因果参考进行比较。我们使用基于 PlanT 2.0 的自动驾驶车辆，在超过 80,000 个合成驾驶场景上进行分析；PlanT 2.0 是一种用于物体级规划的最先进 Transformer 模型。结果表明，注意力提供的是物体相关性的相关关系指示，而非因果指示。基于注意力的相关性估计方法不能一致地识别所有相关物体，并倾向于错误地将对车辆驾驶影响很小或没有影响的物体归类为相关。特别是，结果表明注意力可以在简单的单车道直线道路场景中有效识别相关物体，但在更复杂的设置中（如多车道道路和交叉口）其准确性会下降。这些发现凸显了注意力作为物体相关性代理的局限性，呼吁提出能够准确捕捉单个物体对车辆驾驶的因果影响并将其与相关关系关联区分开来的新方法。

## Abstract
Attention has become one of the most widely adopted solutions for estimating the relevance of surrounding objects and interpreting an autonomous vehicle's driving decisions. However, its ability to capture the causal impact of individual input elements on an AI model's output has long been discussed in the literature, particularly in the Natural Language Processing (NLP) domain. This work brings this discussion into the autonomous driving domain by analyzing the accuracy of attention in estimating the impact of an input object on an autonomous vehicle's driving, i.e., its relevance. To this end, we compare different attention-based relevance estimation methods against a causal reference of object relevance obtained through counterfactual analysis. We perform our analysis on more than 80,000 synthetic driving scenes using an autonomous vehicle based on PlanT 2.0, a state-of-the-art Transformer-based model for object-level planning. Results show that attention provides a correlational rather than causal indication of object relevance. Attention-based relevance estimation methods do not consistently identify all relevant objects and tend to incorrectly classify as relevant objects with little or no impact on the vehicle's driving. In particular, results show that attention can effectively identify relevant objects in simple single-lane, straight-road scenarios, but its accuracy degrades in more complex settings, such as multi-lane roads and intersections. These findings highlight the limitations of attention as a proxy for object relevance, calling for novel methods able to accurately capture the causal impact of individual objects on the vehicle's driving and distinguish it from correlational associations.
