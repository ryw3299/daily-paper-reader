## 摘要
端到端驾驶模型已取得显著进展，但在复杂交通中实现可靠的闭环驾驶仍具挑战性。除了改进单个模型之外，系统级编排通过协调驾驶能力与计算提供了一种互补方法。我们提出 ROSE-Drive（Runtime Orchestration and System Evolution，运行时编排与系统演化），一个与模型无关的框架，可联合编排驾驶执行与计算。它协调冻结的驾驶模型与驾驶技能、感知工具和反馈控制器。一个蒸馏的视觉-语言模型（VLM）提出驾驶任务与场景关系。共享物理状态与持久任务支持能力组合、任务连续性和自适应调度。编排器跨控制通道融合模型与技能输出，通过交接维持任务责任，并利用执行反馈选择控制器、复用有效运动参考以及调度模型推理。离线修订利用执行记录，通过稳定的任务接口改进技能及其协调。后续系统阶段继承这些修订，同时模型、感知和 VLM 权重保持固定。在 Bench2Drive v0.0.3、Bench2Drive v0.0.4 和 Fail2Drive 上使用四个驾驶模型进行的评估显示了在目标基准修订前累积的能力与迁移。在留出集 Longest6 上，随着系统阶段推进，AutoMoT 的驾驶分数（DS）从 25 升至 52，SimLingo 从 22 升至 47，而两个系统在超过 71% 的控制步骤上跳过新的模型推理。

## Abstract
End-to-end driving models have made substantial progress, yet reliable closed-loop driving in complex traffic remains challenging. Beyond improving individual models, system-level orchestration offers a complementary approach by coordinating driving capabilities and computation. We introduce ROSE-Drive (Runtime Orchestration and System Evolution), a model-agnostic framework that jointly orchestrates driving execution and computation. It coordinates frozen driving models with driving skills, perception tools, and feedback controllers. A distilled vision-language model (VLM) proposes driving tasks and scene relations. Shared physical state and persistent tasks support capability composition, task continuity, and adaptive scheduling. The orchestrator combines model and skill outputs across control channels, maintains task responsibility through handover, and uses execution feedback to select controllers, reuse valid motion references, and schedule model inference. Offline revisions use execution records to refine skills and their coordination through a stable task interface. Successive system stages inherit these revisions while model, perception, and VLM weights remain fixed. Evaluations with four driving models on Bench2Drive v0.0.3, Bench2Drive v0.0.4, and Fail2Drive show accumulated capability and transfer before target-benchmark revision. On held-out Longest6, driving score (DS) rises from 25 to 52 for AutoMoT and from 22 to 47 for SimLingo across system stages, while both systems skip new model inference on over 71% of control steps.
