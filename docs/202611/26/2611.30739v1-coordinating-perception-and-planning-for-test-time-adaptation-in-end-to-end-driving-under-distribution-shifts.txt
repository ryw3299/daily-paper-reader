Abstract
End-to-end autonomous driving has achieved significant
milestones, yet its reliance on the i.i.d. assumption renders it
vulnerable to real-world distribution shifts. While Test-Time
Adaptation (TTA) offers a promising paradigm for on-the-
fly enhancement, its application to driving is hindered by
two barriers: (i) multi-task adaptation dependency, where
updates to upstream perception also affect downstream plan-
ning, while perception confidence alone does not directly
assess these downstream effects; and (ii) continuous adap-
tation instability, where recursive online updates over long
horizons cause compounding parameter drift. To address
these challenges, we propose P2-TTA, a plug-and-play TTA
framework for the continual adaptation of end-to-end driv-
ing models. P2-TTA coordinates adaptation through a joint
objective combining perception entropy minimization with
a retrospective temporal planning consistency objective de-
rived from observed ego motion. To stabilize online updates,
we use an EMA teacher as a moving parameter reference
and reject candidate updates that increase historical plan-
ning loss. Extensive experiments across multiple models
demonstrate that, by updating an average of 0.11‰ of pa-
rameters with an average adaptation time of 19.9 ms on dif-
ferent backbones, P2-TTA achieves average recovery rates of
36.4%, 30.0%, and 9.3% for L2, collision rate, and PDMS,
respectively, under severe distribution shifts. Under a shared
perception–planning objective, P2-TTA achieves the highest
L2 and collision recovery among the compared TTA base-
lines on UniAD.
1. Introduction
End-to-end autonomous driving has emerged as a prevalent
paradigm, demonstrating remarkable proficiency and gener-
alization potential across diverse driving scenarios [4, 13].
However, the performance of such models relies heavily on
the assumption that test and training data are independently
and identically distributed (i.i.d.), which rarely holds in prac-
tice [11, 43]. As shown in Figure 1(a), these models degrade
sharply under various real-world distribution shifts; for in-
stance, dense fog, brightness fluctuations, or Gaussian noise
can cause error rates to surge under severe corruption. Exist-
ing approaches address distribution shifts through domain
adaptation (DA) [1, 34] or data augmentation [54, 67], but
typically require access to target-domain data in advance,
additional training, or a predefined corruption model. These
requirements limit their applicability when test conditions
change unexpectedly [10, 33]. This motivates equipping
end-to-end autonomous driving models with the ability to
adapt online at test time, requiring neither labeled data nor
offline retraining.
Test-time adaptation (TTA) offers a compelling alterna-
tive by updating models on the fly using only unlabeled
test samples [31, 53]. Early fully TTA (FTTA) methods
adapt the model via lightweight self-supervised objectives
[26, 42, 59]. Continual TTA (ConTTA) methods further ex-
tend this by continuing to update model parameters across
successive batches of test samples, introducing mechanisms
to prevent forgetting [20, 39, 51]. Many established TTA
methods have been developed for visual recognition, particu-
larly image classification, where adaptation is usually guided
by task-specific prediction signals. Applying such methods
to end-to-end driving additionally requires considering the
influence of perceptual updates on planning and the temporal
dependence of successive observations.
Adapting end-to-end driving models at test time poses
two fundamental challenges for existing TTA methods. (i)
Multi-task adaptation dependency. Existing entropy-based
TTA methods primarily target image classification, where
prediction confidence guides adaptation [35, 36, 53]. In
end-to-end driving, upstream perception and downstream
planning are coupled: degraded perceptual representations
under distribution shifts can propagate to planning outputs,
causing predicted ego motion to deviate from subsequently
observed vehicle motion. (ii) Continuous adaptation insta-
bility. Unlike conventional image classification, where test
samples are often treated as independent prediction instances,

To address these challenges, we propose Perception–
Planning Test-Time Adaptation (P2-TTA), a plug-and-play
continual adaptation framework for end-to-end driving, en-

inference. To coordinate these coupled tasks, P2-TTA com-

in a joint adaptation objective. The two signals provide com-

served ego motion. To mitigate unstable parameter changes,
P2-TTA projects candidate updates around an EMA param-
eter reference and accepts them only when a retrospective
planning consistency criterion improves. Together, these
mechanisms constrain parameter changes during online adap-
tation. Extensive experiments on nuScenes and NAVSIM
under various corruptions demonstrate that P2-TTA gener-
alizes across various end-to-end models of diverse architec-
tures, achieving the highest L2 and collision recovery among
the compared TTA baselines on UniAD. Moreover, ablation
studies further validate the contribution of each component,
while efficiency analysis shows low additional adaptation
overhead. In summary, our contributions are as follows:
• We propose P2-TTA, a plug-and-play framework for end-
to-end driving that enables diverse pretrained models to
adapt at test time under severe distribution shifts.
• We formulate a joint self-supervised objective that inte-
grates upstream perception entropy with retrospective tem-
poral planning consistency, guiding model updates toward
more reliable end-to-end driving under distribution shifts.
• We introduce a teacher-student architecture that combines
EMA parameter anchoring with per-step update verifica-
tion to constrain parameter changes during adaptation.
• We demonstrate that P2-TTA updates an average of 0.11‰
of parameters with an average adaptation time of 19.9 ms,
achieving average recovery rates of 36.4%, 30.0%, and
9.3% for L2, collision rate, and PDMS, respectively, under
distribution shift.
2. Related Work
Domain Adaptation. DA addresses distribution shift by
aligning features between the labeled source domain and the
unlabeled target domain [14, 27, 45, 62], typically through
moment matching [40], adversarial domain discrimination
[15, 18, 52], or optimal transport [7], achieving strong per-
formance when target data is available at training time. How-
ever, DA methods require access to target domain data during
training and often necessitate retraining—making them im-
practical when the test distribution is unknown in advance.
Test-Time Training. Test-Time Training (TTT) adapts
models using self-supervised objectives on unlabeled test in-
puts, without requiring target-domain data in advance [8, 49].
Traditional approaches employ auxiliary tasks, such as rota-
tion prediction [49] or noise-contrastive learning [37], which
often require modifications to the training pipeline. More
recently, Centaur [44] extends TTT to end-to-end driving
by minimizing cluster entropy over direction-grouped can-
didate trajectories. However, existing approaches primarily
rely on task-specific adaptation signals without explicitly
accounting for the coupling between upstream perception
and downstream planning.
Fully Test-Time Adaptation. FTTA requires no mod-
ification to training, updating model parameters using un-
supervised signals at test time [6, 35, 41, 53, 65]. Entropy
minimization over model predictions has emerged as the
dominant paradigm, with subsequent work introducing vari-
ous mechanisms to improve update reliability and prevent

Continual Test-Time Adaptation. ConTTA extends
FTTA to settings where the model must update over a se-

getting [36, 47, 57]. To prevent error accumulation, recent
methods maintain an exponential moving average (EMA)
model to generate stable pseudo-targets and stochastically
restore a subset of parameters toward their pretrained values
to preserve source knowledge [3, 46, 50, 61]. While these
methods address sequential distribution shifts, they do not
necessarily provide a planning-related supervision signal tai-
lored to end-to-end driving. In particular, preserving param-
eter stability and improving downstream trajectory quality
are distinct objectives that should be evaluated separately.

4. Methodology
To address the cross-task and cross-frame requirements in
Eq. 3, P2-TTA introduces two complementary components.
Taking into account the coupling relationship between up-
stream perception and downstream planning, P2-TTA con-
structs a joint self-supervised objective Ljoint to enable uni-
fied cross-task coordination throughout the adaptation pro-
cess. A teacher-student framework additionally constrains
parameter updates and verifies them using temporal plan-
ning consistency. P2-TTA updates only the affine parameters
(γ, β) of selected LayerNorm modules. Given the architec-
tural differences across driving models, the LayerNorm mod-
ules selected for adaptation vary accordingly. Model-specific
LayerNorm configurations are detailed in Appendix F.
4.1. Joint Objective for Perception–Planning Coor-
dination
End-to-end autonomous driving models are inherently multi-
task systems with highly interdependent components [5, 17,
23]. During supervised training, task-specific labels provide
reference signals for optimizing perception and planning.
At test time, however, such labels are unavailable, motivat-
ing a self-supervised adaptation objective that guides online
parameter updates using unlabeled observations. While min-
imizing perception entropy encourages confident predictions,
it does not directly assess the effect of an update on down-
stream planning. We therefore combine this upstream signal
with a temporal planning consistency loss based on observed
ego motion, enabling coordinated adaptation of perception

4.2. Teacher-Student Architecture for Cross-frame
Stability

process where environmental shifts are not isolated events
but persistent perturbations. Despite the synergetic guid-
ance imposed by our joint objective Ljoint, adaptation over
continuous test streams faces the risk of error accumula-
tion. Since each update is conditioned on previously adapted
states, minor gradient biases at a single frame can gradu-
ally compound, leading to severe parameter drift or even
catastrophic model collapse.

5. Experiments
5.1. Experimental Setup
We evaluate P2-TTA on nuScenes and NAVSIM using offi-
cially released pretrained weights under two distinct adap-
tation protocols. On nuScenes, parameters are updated se-
quentially across recorded frames within a scene and reset
at scene boundaries; the observed ego state is available only
up to the current frame, and the evaluated planner does not
control the recorded motion. We report L2 trajectory error
and collision rate averaged over the 1s, 2s, and 3s predic-
tion horizons. On NAVSIM, each independently evaluated
sample uses three observations for a single adaptation step
before its predicted plan is assessed by a non-reactive roll-
out, and parameters are reset between samples. We report
PDMS for NAVSIM. In both protocols, pobs
t
is drawn from
recorded ego-state observations available no later than time t,
without using future trajectory labels for adaptation. Recov-
ery rate (RR) measures the fraction of corruption-induced
degradation recovered relative to the unadapted clean and
noisy references; mean RR averages per-model rates with
positive clean-to-noisy degradation. Unless otherwise spec-
ified, adaptation uses Adam with learning rate η = 0.01,
EMA momentum α = 0.8, projection radius r = 0.005, and
loss weight λ = 1. The default hyperparameters are shared
across corruption types and remain fixed at test time. All
experiments are conducted on NVIDIA A800 GPUs. All
reported results are averaged over three runs with different
random seeds.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we presented P2-TTA, a plug-and-play contin-
ual adaptation framework for end-to-end autonomous driv-
ing, designed for cross-task and cross-frame adaptation. Its
joint objective coordinates perception entropy minimization
with temporal planning consistency, with ablations showing
complementary gains in performance. An EMA-centered
projection and consistency-based acceptance mechanism
constrain individual parameter updates. Extensive evalua-
tions across diverse driving architectures demonstrate that P2-
TTA effectively mitigates severe distribution shifts, achiev-
ing substantial reductions in displacement errors and colli-
sion rates with low overhead.

F.2. Model-Specific Adaptation on nuScenes
All models use observed ego motion for temporal consis-
tency; parameter counts are in Table S17.
UniAD links perception and planning through BEV-query
transformers. Entropy uses ten-class sigmoid scores from six
classification branches; selected LayerNorm modules cover
the perception transformer, ego-state embedding, classifica-
tion branches, and planning attention/fusion.
ST-P3 uses dense spatiotemporal BEV features. Entropy
uses softmax vehicle/pedestrian segmentation probabilities;
adaptation targets BatchNorm affine parameters in segmen-
tation heads and planning channel-reduction blocks, with
running statistics fixed.
SparseDrive, DistillDrive, and nuScenes Diffusion-
Drive use sparse perception with ego planning, multi-mode
planning distillation, and diffusion trajectory decoding, re-
spectively. Entropy uses sigmoid object scores from six
detection refinement stages. Selected LayerNorm modules
lie in detection classification branches, the planning-anchor
encoder, and the respective planning classification branches.
CoIRL-AD combines imitation and reinforcement learn-
ing in a latent world model. Its imitation pathway lacks
a trained probabilistic perception head, so adaptation uses
temporal consistency alone. LayerNorm parameters in way-
point attention are adapted, with the reinforcement-learning
branch frozen.
F.3. Implementation Details of TTA Baselines
The main baselines share our objective and loss weight
(Eq. 4), selected UniAD LayerNorm parameters, and per-
scene reset rule.
Perception entropy uses ten-class sig-
moid scores from six classification branches, averaged over
branches, queries, and classes; temporal consistency uses
recomputed historical plans and observed ego motion.
Tent [53] directly minimizes the shared objective.
EATA [35] retains reliability/diversity filtering and EWC;
EATA–E removes EWC. SAR [36] retains reliability
filtering, sharpness-aware updates, and model recovery.
CoTTA [57] retains its EMA teacher and stochastic source
restoration.

I. Limitations and Future Work
Future work will study when to trigger, pause, and resume
adaptation using unlabeled signals, and how to adjust up-
date step sizes dynamically to balance adaptation speed and
stability under changing conditions.
