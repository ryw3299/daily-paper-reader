## 摘要
端到端自动驾驶已取得重要进展，但其对独立同分布（i.i.d.）假设的依赖使其易受真实世界分布偏移的影响。尽管测试时自适应（TTA）为即时增强提供了一种有前景的范式，但其在驾驶中的应用受到两个障碍的阻碍：(i) 多任务自适应依赖，即上游感知的更新也会影响下游规划，而仅凭感知置信度无法直接评估这些下游影响；(ii) 持续自适应不稳定性，即长时程上的递归在线更新会导致参数漂移累积。为解决这些挑战，我们提出 P2-TTA，一种用于端到端驾驶模型持续自适应的即插即用 TTA 框架。P2-TTA 通过联合目标协调自适应，该目标将感知熵最小化与由观测自车运动导出的回溯时序规划一致性目标相结合。为稳定在线更新，我们使用 EMA 教师作为移动参数参考，并拒绝会增加历史规划损失的候选更新。在多个模型上的大量实验表明，在不同骨干网络上平均仅更新 0.11‰ 的参数、平均自适应时间为 19.9 ms 的情况下，P2-TTA 在严重分布偏移下对 L2、碰撞率和 PDMS 分别取得 36.4%、30.0% 和 9.3% 的平均恢复率。在共享感知-规划目标下，P2-TTA 在 UniAD 上相比所比较的 TTA 基线取得了最高的 L2 和碰撞恢复。

## Abstract
End-to-end autonomous driving has achieved significant milestones, yet its reliance on the i.i.d. assumption renders it vulnerable to real-world distribution shifts. While Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) offers a promising paradigm for on-the-fly enhancement, its application to driving is hindered by two barriers: (i) multi-task adaptation dependency, where updates to upstream perception also affect downstream planning, while perception confidence alone does not directly assess these downstream effects; and (ii) continuous adaptation instability, where recursive online updates over long horizons cause compounding parameter drift. To address these challenges, we propose P2-TTA, a plug-and-play TTA framework for the continual adaptation of end-to-end driving models. P2-TTA coordinates adaptation through a joint objective combining perception entropy minimization with a retrospective temporal planning consistency objective derived from observed ego motion. To stabilize online updates, we use an EMA teacher as a moving parameter reference and reject candidate updates that increase historical planning loss. Extensive experiments across multiple models demonstrate that, by updating an average of 0.11 per mille of parameters with an average adaptation time of 19.9 ms on different backbones, P2-TTA achieves average recovery rates of 36.4%, 30.0%, and 9.3% for L2, collision rate, and PDMS, respectively, under severe distribution shifts. Under a shared perception-planning objective, P2-TTA achieves the highest L2 and collision recovery among the compared TTA baselines on UniAD.
