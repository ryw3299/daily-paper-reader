# XMind: Thinking Twice via Cross-Memory Reasoning for LLM-Based Autonomous Driving 

Zuyi Guo 1, Xianjie Dai 1, Lei Yang 2, Xiaocong Zhao 3, Jingguang Li 1, Kitae Jang 1, and Heye Huang 1,†

1Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology 

2Nanyang Technological University 

3Tongji University 

Abstract — Memory-augmented large language model (LLM) driving agents can reuse past experience, yet previously learned corrections often fail to transfer when traffic conditions change. A key limitation is that existing memories mainly record observed outcomes, providing little evidence about alternative actions. We introduce XMind, a training-free framework that enables LLM-based driving to “Think Twice” through cross-memory reasoning. XMind integrates working memory of recent traffic states with episodic memory of critical risk events. The agent first makes an initial decision from the current context and then cross-checks it against retrieved historical experience. If a conflict arises, it makes a second decision guided by the retrieved evidence. To construct reliable episodic memory, XMind evaluates alternative actions through counterfactual rollouts under perturbed traffic conditions and stores the verified outcomes as structured risk capsules. These capsules are retrieved by jointly matching spatial risk patterns and scenario semantics. Across closed-loop highway simulations, XMind achieves success rates of 83.3–93.3%, ex-ceeding the strong baseline by 6.6 percentage points on average. Evaluation on K-Risk further demonstrates lower decision risk on unseen real-world high-risk interactions. Ablation studies confirm the complementary benefits of working memory, risk– text retrieval, and counterfactual verification. Code is available at https://github.com/xiangxiangliuwo/XMind .

Index Terms — Autonomous driving, large language models, cross-memory reasoning, counterfactual reasoning. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Recent advances have substantially improved autonomous driving performance in normal traffic scenarios, yet reliable decision-making in long-tail scenarios remains challenging. Because events such as abrupt braking, cut-ins, and dense multi-vehicle interactions are rare, the experience gained from such events can be particularly valuable. For large language model (LLM) driving agents, external memory provides a way to reuse such experience without updating model param-eters [1, 2]. Reliable experience reuse, however, raises two questions: what should be remembered from a past risk event, and when should that memory influence the current decision? 

Existing LLM driving agents retrieve past experience and reflections to support decision-making [1, 3, 4], while RE-SPOND [5] further organizes memories using structured risk patterns. However, similar scenarios or risk layouts do not nec-essarily require the same maneuver. More importantly, existing memories mainly record the outcome of the executed action,

Counter-factual memories such as CounterMem [6] verify corrective actions and their applicability, but retain little information about the other actions available at the same critical state. Safety-critical driving therefore requires multi-action evidence that distinguishes recoverable, dangerous, and uncertain alter-natives under changing traffic conditions. A second challenge is deciding when retrieved experience should affect online reasoning. Existing memory-augmented agents typically use retrieved experience whenever a match is found, allowing weakly related memory to interfere with otherwise reasonable decisions. This motivates a selective mechanism in which memory is used only when it provides evidence that challenges the agent’s initial choice. To address these challenges, we propose XMind, a training-free cross-memory reasoning framework for LLM-based au-arXiv:2611.32209v1 [cs.AI] 24 Nov 2026 tonomous driving. XMind combines short-term working mem-ory of recent traffic states with long-term episodic memory of critical risk events, as illustrated in Fig. 1. In the offline phase, candidate actions at critical states are evaluated through counterfactual rollouts under traffic perturbations and stored as structured risk capsules. During online decision-making, the LLM first makes an initial decision from working memory and then cross-checks it against retrieved episodic evidence. When the two disagree, XMind triggers a second, evidence-guided decision, allowing the system to “Think Twice” while keeping the underlying LLM frozen. The primary contributions of this paper are as follows:  

> •

We propose XMind, a cross-memory reasoning frame-work that lets an LLM driving agent “Think Twice”: its initial decision from working memory is cross-checked against episodic risk memory, and disagreement triggers an evidence-guided revision.  

> •

We construct a counterfactually verified episodic risk memory, whose risk capsules record recoverable, danger-ous, and uncertain actions at decision-critical states and are retrieved through joint risk–text matching.  

> •

We validate XMind through closed-loop simulations, open-loop evaluation on K-Risk [7], and ablation studies, showing higher success rates than strong baselines and reduced risk on recorded high-risk events. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. LLM-based Driving Agents 

LLM driving frameworks use different representations and retrieval mechanisms to reuse past experience. DiLu [1] re-trieves prior reasoning traces and actions from scenario de-scriptions, while Agent-Driver [3] separates commonsense and driving memories. RAG-Driver [8] learns a shared retrieval space for visual and control features, LeapVAD [9] uses action-aware contrastive representations, and LiloDriver [10] incrementally associates scenarios with planning strategies for context-aware decision support. In contrast, XMind combines working memory with episodic risk memory and uses coun-terfactually verified evidence to revise decisions only when retrieved experience conflicts with the initial choice. Risk information can also be incorporated into the retrieval space. Driver risk field (DRF) [11] estimates perceived risk from a state-dependent spatial field combined with environ-mental costs. Driving safety field captures coupled driver– vehicle–road interactions [12] and yields dynamic risk maps when combined with the intentions of surrounding vehi-cles [13]. RiskNet [14] couples interaction fields with proba-bilistic behavior prediction to forecast risk in long-tail scenar-ios. SafeDrive [15] extends DRF to quantify coupled multi-agent risks for LLM driving reasoning, while RESPOND [5] encodes ego-centric spatial risk in a discrete matrix and retrieves experience through exact pattern matching. 

B. Experience Memory and Reflection 

Memory systems for language agents differ in the knowl-edge they store and the signals used to update it. Reason-ingBank [16] uses LLM assessments of trajectory outcomes to extract structured strategy entries from both successes and failures. EvolveR [17] maintains a principle repository through semantic deduplication and utility scoring, and combines ex-perience reuse with reinforcement learning policy updates. ECHO [18] rewrites trajectories in hindsight and compresses them into reusable experience. Driving memory also varies in its temporal scope and role in decision making. AD-Memo [19] retains information about objects and events within a driving episode to support subsequent decisions. In this work, XMind focuses on building knowledge for handling risks across episodes. It stores action outcomes and supporting rollout evidence at specific decision states, allowing the agent to consult the evidence behind past experience when making subsequent decisions. 

C. Counterfactual Evaluation for Driving Decisions 

Evaluating driving decisions in high-risk scenarios requires assessing not only whether collisions can be avoided, but also how alternative actions affect future outcomes. Nishiyama et al. [20] use counterfactual simulation to distinguish avoidable from unavoidable planner failures. FREA [21] approximates the ego vehicle’s feasible region and constrains adversar-ial background behavior, while WoTE [22] predicts action-conditioned futures with a BEV world model and evaluates candidate trajectories using a reward model. Recent work further studies counterfactual prediction in driving world mod-els, highlighting the difficulty of estimating futures under alternative ego actions [23]. Counterfactual evaluation can also provide supervision and reusable experience. CF-VLA [24] constructs counterfactual correction data through rollout, filtering, and labeling, while C-CoT [25] uses a meta-action evaluation tree to supervise safety reasoning. These methods mainly use counterfactual outcomes for training or correction. In contrast, XMind stores multi-action evaluation results as persistent episodic risk memory, allowing verified evidence to be retrieved across episodes and used to reconsider subsequent driving decisions. The evaluation follows three questions: (Q1) Does the mem-ory improve safety and efficiency over relevant baselines? (Q2) Does accumulation improve decisions on unseen real-data risk events? (Q3) Which mechanisms explain the difference? 

A. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate XMind in Highway-env [28], whose dis-crete meta-action space is A = {LANE_LEFT , IDLE ,

LANE_RIGHT , FASTER , SLOWER } (Section III-A). The main experiments use three configurations with different traffic complexity: four lanes with density 2.5, five lanes with density 2.5, and five lanes with density 3.0. Each configuration is evaluated over 3 rounds of 30 episodes with different random seeds. Particularly, Q1 and Q3 use different test seeds to broaden the coverage of randomly generated traffic, so their results are not directly comparable. Each episode lasts 30 decision steps and succeeds if the ego vehicle completes all steps without collision. All experiments use GPT-6-Astra as the LLM backbone without fine-tuning. B. Q1: Baseline Comparison 

We compare XMind with DiLu [1], the single-agent variant of KoMA [4], and RESPOND [5]. Each method builds mem-ory with its reflection mechanism on shared seeds excluded from testing, and the memory is fixed during evaluation. 

Safety metrics. Table I summarizes the comparison. XMind achieves success rates of 93.3%, 90.0%, and 83.3%, ranking first in all three configurations. Relative to RESPOND, its success rate is higher by approximately 6.6 to 6.7 percentage points, and its mean success steps increase across configu-rations. With five lanes and a traffic density of 3.0, XMind exceeds DiLu and KoMA in success rate by 36.6 and 23.3 percentage points, respectively. Within the fixed horizon of 30 decision steps, XMind completes a greater proportion of episodes without collision, with mean episode lengths closer to the horizon limit. With five lanes, increasing traffic density from 2.5 to 3.0 reduces the mean minimum TTC for all methods, indicating tighter temporal margins in denser traffic. XMind records mean minimum TTC values of 7.19, 5.62, and 4.44 s across the three configurations. Its successful trajectories therefore retain temporal margins of several seconds on average at their most critical approach points. 

Efficiency and cost. Across the three traffic configurations, XMind’s mean speed ranges from 10.74 to 11.65 m/s, gen-erally below DiLu and KoMA and close to RESPOND. Although DiLu and KoMA achieve higher mean speeds, both have lower success rates and success steps than XMind. XMind does not lead in speed across all configurations, but it completes more episodes without collision and executes more decision steps on average. Compared with RESPOND, which also incorporates risk information, XMind achieves better episode completion at similar speed levels. We presented XMind, a cross-memory reasoning framework for LLM-based autonomous driving. It combines working memory with counterfactually verified episodic risk memory to support “Think Twice” decision-making. At critical states, XMind evaluates alternative actions and records whether they are recoverable, dangerous, or uncertain. When retrieved evi-dence disagrees with the initial decision, the agent reconsiders it with this evidence. In closed-loop highway simulation, XMind achieves the highest success rate in all three configura-tions without updating the LLM. Ablations confirm the bene-fits of working memory, risk–text retrieval, and counterfactual verification. On recorded K-Risk events, simulator-derived ex-perience reduces decision risk on highD and NGSIM, although its benefit remains limited on the extreme subset. Overall, XMind shows that verified past experience can complement current traffic context for more reliable LLM-based driving.
