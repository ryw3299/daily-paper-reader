Abstract
Sparse world models for 4D semantic occupancy fore-
casting represent scenes with a small set of evolving queries
to efficiently predict future geometry and semantics for au-
tonomous driving. However, existing sparse world mod-
els face a bottleneck in using historical temporal evidence
for long-horizon forecasting.
They rely on scene states
to carry historical information implicitly, allowing errors
to propagate and accumulate across successive forecast-
ing steps and causing geometric and semantic deviations.
To address this limitation, we present MemAnchorWorld,
a sparse occupancy world model that explicitly retrieves
relevant historical geometric and semantic evidence for
4D semantic occupancy forecasting. Inspired by the von
Restorff effect, we introduce a forecast-time-conditioned
hierarchical dual-path memory-anchor retrieval mecha-
nism. Guided by the effective age and semantic compatibil-
ity of historical information, it combines query-level can-
didate selection with point-level memory-anchor retrieval
to provide historical evidence for sparse points at different
forecast times. To better use memory anchors and exist-
ing features, a point-level residual adapter jointly gated
by the task and forecast time separately controls resid-
ual corrections to semantics, occupancy confidence, and
spatial positions. On Occ3D-nuScenes, MemAnchorWorld
achieves an average mIoU of 13.55% and an average IoU
of 22.84% across the 1–3 s forecasting horizons, attaining
state-of-the-art performance on both metrics. Project page:
https://memanchorworld.github.io/.

• We propose a forecast-time-conditioned hierarchical

2. Related Work
2.1. Camera-Based Semantic Occupancy
Camera-based methods infer scene geometry and semantics
from visual cues, offering lower sensor costs and flexible
deployment [18, 19, 25, 29, 34]. MonoScene [3] recov-
ers scene geometry and semantics from a monocular im-
age through 2D-to-3D feature projection. VoxFormer [19]
uses depth estimation to construct sparse voxel queries
for visible occupied regions, followed by feature propaga-
tion for scene completion. For surround-view scenes, Sur-
roundOcc [34] aggregates multi-camera features through
multi-scale 2D–3D attention to construct a 3D volumetric
representation, whereas TPVFormer [13] encodes 3D space
using three mutually orthogonal planes to reduce compu-
tation and storage costs. Other camera-based approaches
include voxel occupancy in OccNet [29], compact 3D en-
coding in COTR [25], video-based self-supervision in Self-
Occ [14], and panoptic occupancy in PanoOcc [32].
To reduce redundant computation in unoccupied re-
gions, later studies explore sparse scene representations.
SparseOcc [23] combines sparse 3D reconstruction with
mask-guided sampling.
OPUS [31] directly predicts oc-
cupied point locations and semantic classes from learnable
queries. Other methods use parameterized geometric prim-
itives to describe local space. GaussianFormer-2 [15] com-
bines probabilistic Gaussian superposition with semantic
mixture modeling to make scene representations more com-
pact. For temporal modeling, GaussianWorld [42] decom-
poses scene evolution into ego-motion alignment, dynamic-
object motion, and completion of newly observed regions,
and updates its Gaussian scene state accordingly.
2.2. 4D Occupancy Forecasting and World Models
4D occupancy forecasting predicts future scene occupancy
from historical observations [17]. Cam4DOcc [24] estab-
lishes a benchmark for camera-based 4D occupancy fore-
casting, while UniOcc [33] provides a unified benchmark
for occupancy forecasting and current-frame prediction. Ef-
ficientOCF [35] decomposes 3D occupancy into bird’s-eye-
view occupancy and a height representation, and associates
instances through predicted flow. I2-World [20] uses intra-
scene and inter-scene tokenization for efficient dynamic 4D
forecasting. OccWorld [40] jointly forecasts future occu-
pancy and ego trajectories from discrete scene tokens with
a spatiotemporal generative Transformer. DOME [8] uses
trajectory-conditioned spatiotemporal diffusion to generate
future scenes. PreWorld [18] combines 2D rendering pre-
training with 3D occupancy supervision to recurrently pre-
dict future occupancy and ego trajectories.
Recent studies apply sparse scene representations to fu-
ture forecasting [4, 5, 7, 41]. SparseWorld [4] represents
scenes with dynamic sparse queries and predicts future oc-
cupancy through ego-state-conditioned updates and posi-
tion regression. DLWM [41] represents scenes with sparse
3D Gaussians and learns Gaussian-flow-guided latent dy-
namics for future occupancy forecasting. However, recur-
rent sparse models may gradually lose local geometric and
semantic information from earlier observations as scene
states evolve. MemAnchorWorld addresses this limitation
by retrieving memory anchors from historical sparse queries
conditioned on the target forecast time and spatial location,
then refining future occupancy predictions through residual
corrections jointly gated by task and forecast time.

3.2. Framework Overview
As shown in Fig. 2, MemAnchorWorld retrieves memory
anchors to refine sparse future predictions and produce 4D
semantic occupancy. The perception backbone first encodes
surround-view images into a sparse scene state Z0. Condi-
tioned on the ego state, the forecasting backbone recurrently
updates scene queries to generate predictions Bh at each
forecast horizon. The forecast-time-conditioned hierarchi-
cal dual-path memory-anchor retrieval module (Sec. 3.3)
constructs a historical memory bank M from queries in
earlier frames. Before retrieval at each forecast horizon,
it uses ego poses to align historical points in a common
coordinate frame, then applies object motion compensa-
tion to extrapolate them to the target forecast time. Using
the corresponding scene queries and predicted points as re-
trieval cues and conditioned on the forecast-time embed-
ding, the module considers historical age, semantic reliabil-
ity, and geometric–semantic relevance. It first selects can-
didate historical queries through geometric–semantic dual-
path coarse retrieval, then performs memory-anchor re-
trieval among their associated points and aggregates the re-
trieved features and semantics as correction evidence. Fi-
nally, the point-level residual adapter jointly gated by task
and forecast time (Sec. 3.4) fuses the retrieved evidence
with the current prediction representation and forecast-time
information. It generates separate residual corrections for
semantics, occupancy confidence, and spatial position, with
independent gates modulating the strength of each correc-
tion.
Sparse occupancy decoding converts the corrected
point-level outputs into semantic occupancy grids bYh at
each forecast horizon.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Details
Dataset.
We
conduct
experiments
on
Occ3D-
nuScenes [28], which provides visibility-aware semantic
occupancy annotations for nuScenes [2].
Following the
forecasting protocol of sparse world models, we use 700
scenes for training and 150 for validation.
Each scene
lasts approximately 20 s, with annotated frames at 0.5 s
intervals. The evaluation region spans [−40, 40] m along
both horizontal axes and [−1, 5.4] m vertically.
It is
discretized into a 200 × 200 × 16 grid with 0.4 m voxels
and 17 occupied semantic classes plus free space.
The
forecasting backbone observes the current frame and four
preceding frames over 2 s, then rolls out six future states at
0.5 s intervals. We evaluate the predictions at 1, 2, and 3 s.
Implementation
Details.
We
use
a
pretrained
SparseWorld-R50 backbone [4], comprising a ResNet-50
image encoder [9] and an FPN neck [21] for six surround-
view images at 256×704 resolution. Its six decoding layers
expand each query into (1, 4, 16, 24, 32, 48) points, and its
query schedule assigns 720 queries to the current state and
(60, 60, 60, 60, 40, 40) to the six future steps. We freeze the
perception and forecasting backbone while optimizing Me-
mAnchorWorld’s memory retrieval and residual-adaptation
modules with the objective in Appendix B.3, Eq. (B.15).
In this implementation, Kmem = 3 historical slots are
centered at t−2.5, t−3.5, and t−4.5 s, with a 0.35 s match-
ing tolerance and a fixed effective-age limit amax = 8 s.
Each slot contributes at most 256 queries, giving a memory
of at most 768 entries. Both retrieval levels use a 6 m ra-
dius and retain up to eight candidates per path or point-level
head; implementation details are provided in Appendix B.2.
Evaluation Metrics.
For occupancy forecasting, we
report semantic mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) over
the 17 occupied classes and class-agnostic occupied-space
IoU at 1, 2, and 3 seconds. For motion planning, we report
the Euclidean L2 displacement error and the box collision
rate at the same horizons, together with their averages.

At 1, 2, and 3 s, we evaluate historical complementarity
(Hist. compl.), correction of wrong base predictions with
correct history (WC), and retention of correct base predic-
tions with wrong history (CW). Metric definitions are pro-
vided in Appendix C.4.
Complementary Historical Evidence. Table 3 reports
rates of 25.25%, 24.29%, and 23.19% at the three horizons,
respectively. Thus, roughly one quarter of points misclas-
sified by the base prediction can retrieve semantically cor-
rect memory anchors from history, providing complemen-
tary semantic evidence for refining the corresponding future
point-level predictions.
Use of Complementary History. Among points with an
incorrect base prediction but a correct historical class, the
adapter corrects 9.57%, 11.12%, and 12.58% at 1, 2, and
3 s, respectively. The correction rate increases with the fore-
cast horizon. These results show that the adapter can use
correct retrieved historical semantics to guide residual cor-
rections, recovering the correct class for some initially mis-
classified points and translating complementary evidence
into effective prediction refinement.

4.5. Visualizations
4D Semantic Occupancy Forecasting. As shown in Fig. 3,
MemAnchorWorld and SparseWorld produce similar occu-
pancy at 0 s because they share the perception backbone. At
1–3 s, SparseWorld misclassifies some road regions within
the red boxes as vehicles, producing false vehicle occu-
pancy, whereas MemAnchorWorld more accurately predicts
the semantic classes of the regions around vehicles. These
results suggest that memory anchors retrieved by MemAn-
chorWorld from historical memory supply class cues for
misclassified regions, enabling gated corrections to reduce
semantic errors in future predictions.

for two occlusion scenes. The two scenes contain six and

anchor retrieval and fusion mechanisms.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we presented MemAnchorWorld, a sparse
occupancy world model addressing the temporal-evidence
bottleneck in long-horizon forecasting. The model gathers
historical evidence through hierarchical dual-path memory-
anchor retrieval and uses a residual adapter jointly gated
by task and forecast time to correct semantics, occupancy
confidence, and spatial positions. Experiments on Occ3D-
nuScenes demonstrate its forecasting and planning perfor-
mance. Our retrieval does not explicitly establish object-
level correspondences across time; future work will explore
stronger motion-aware associations to improve the reliabil-
ity of memory-anchor retrieval in dynamic scenes.
